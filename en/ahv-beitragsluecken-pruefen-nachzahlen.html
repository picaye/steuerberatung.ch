AHV Contribution Gaps: Check Your Pension Account and Pay In Missing Years Within 5 Years

What a contribution gap costs

A full Swiss state pension (AHV/1st pillar) requires 44 complete contribution years, running from 1 January after your 20th birthday to the end of the calendar year before you reach the reference age. Every missing year reduces the pension by 1/44 – about 2.3 per cent – for life. The shortfall cannot later be offset by a higher income (source: Swiss Federal Social Insurance Office, FSO/SVAM).

The FSO distinguishes two situations. An insurance gap arises where a person was not subject to compulsory AHV at all, typically after moving abroad. A contribution gap exists where the person was insured but did not pay throughout – for example after failing to register as a non-employed person with the compensation fund during an extended break. In both cases each missing year reduces the pension by 1/44.

Tip: Order your individual account statement before you finalise plans such as early retirement – a gap can almost always be corrected only while it is still within the limitation period.

The individual account statement: how to spot your gaps

The individual account statement (Individuelles Konto, IK) lists every contribution year and recorded income. It is free and can be ordered at any time from the compensation fund of your canton of residence, online or at the local AHV office. Check that every calendar year since the year after your 20th birthday appears with a contribution entry.

If you find an error – a missing year despite payslips, for instance – you can file a correction request within 30 days of receiving the statement and attach the evidence. Employees are protected: if the employer deducted contributions but failed to pass them on, the employee bears no pension reduction, provided the deductions can be proven (Art. 30ter para. 2 AHVG).

Tip: Do not wait until shortly before retirement. Offices recommend ordering the statement every three to five years and keeping your salary certificates at least that long.

Paying in contributions: only the last five years

Missing contributions can be paid in retroactively – but only within the forfeiture period of Article 16 para. 1 AHVG: five years after the end of the calendar year for which they were owed. Acting in 2026 means you can still pay in 2021, 2020, 2019, 2018 and 2017; every further year of waiting definitively closes one contributable year. The precondition is that you were subject to compulsory insurance in Switzerland during the relevant time.

The procedure runs through your compensation fund, which issues a formal assessment; the amount is payable within 30 days of billing, and default interest applies to late payment. Pay-in years then count normally towards your contribution record – although paying only the minimum contribution as a non-employed person closes the year without raising the average income on which the pension is calculated.

Tip: Each December, check which year is about to become time-barred and pay it in if needed. The door closes permanently, one year at a time.

Who is at risk? Typical sources of gaps

Gaps most often affect people whose income is not continuously subject to employer-remitted contributions: the self-employed who never registered with a compensation fund; those retiring early; recipients of daily sickness and accident allowances (no AHV contributions are levied on such allowances); the long-term unemployed after benefits end; travellers; and students from 1 January after their 20th birthday – up to age 25 they pay only the minimum contribution of CHF 530 (AHV/IV/EO combined, 2026 level).

Part-time work can also leave a hole: anyone employed for less than nine months or less than 50 per cent of the usual workload in a year is subject to a comparison calculation, and may owe additional contributions as a non-employed person.

Non-employed spouses are exempt if their partner is employed within the meaning of the AHVA and pays at least twice the minimum contribution, i.e. CHF 1'060 per year (Fact sheet 2.03, as of 1 January 2026); the same applies to those working in the spouse's business without cash pay. Note that the exemption depends on the partner actually paying – after separation, death, or if the partner has gaps, your own contribution obligation must be clarified immediately.

Tip: After every life change – job loss with allowance payments, a sabbatical, studies – register proactively with your cantonal compensation fund. The non-employed-person registration (form 318.148) usually prevents a gap at no or minimal cost.

Closing gaps later: what the law still allows

Beyond the five-year pay-in, the FSO recognises other routes to repair an incomplete record: contributions paid before age 20 or in the months immediately before reaching the reference age can offset missing years; so-called supplementary years exist for gaps before 1979; and since the AHV 21 reform (2024), contributions paid while working after the reference age can be used for a one-off pension recalculation if the maximum pension has not yet been reached.

These routes have strict conditions and deadlines. The compensation fund reviews your record ex officio when you claim your pension – but do not rely on an old employer's or authority's mistake being noticed automatically.

Tip: If you plan to retire early, use the final months before the reference age: stay insured to month-end or register as non-employed for the remaining months – gaps in the non-time-barred range can still be repaired.

In short

  • Every missing AHV contribution year cuts the pension for life by 1/44 (about 2.3 per cent).
  • The individual account statement is free and shows all contribution years – order one every three to five years.
  • You can pay in only the last five years after the relevant calendar year ends (Art. 16 para. 1 AHVG); in 2026, 2021 is the last pay-in-able year.
  • Allowance recipients, early retirees, the self-employed, students from 20/21 and the long-term unemployed must register with the compensation fund themselves.
  • Non-employed spouses are exempt if their partner pays at least CHF 1'060 in AHV contributions per year.
