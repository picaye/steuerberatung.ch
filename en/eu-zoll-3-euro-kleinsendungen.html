Swiss Online Shops Selling into the EU: Customs Duty on Small Parcels Since July 2026

What changed on 1 July 2026

If you sell from Switzerland into the EU, you know the old rule: parcels with a value of goods up to 150 EUR were duty-free. That exemption no longer exists. On 11 February 2026 the Council of the European Union adopted Regulation (EU) 2026/382, which amends the previous Duty Relief Regulation (EC) No 1186/2009 – with effect from 1 July 2026.

Since then, distance-sale consignments from third countries – thus also from Switzerland – are subject to a flat customs duty of 3 EUR per goods category. "Goods category" means: per item line declared in the customs declaration. A parcel containing three different product groups therefore incurs 9 EUR in duty, not 3.

German customs (zoll.de) and the Austrian Federal Ministry of Finance (bmf.gv.at) confirm the rule in identical terms. Important: this concerns the customs duty – import VAT remains due regardless (the 22-euro tax exemption threshold was already scrapped in 2021).

And there is more to come: on current plans the EU will levy an additional handling fee ("Union Handling Fee") of 2 EUR per product category on distance-sales consignments from 1 November 2026 – independent of goods value, non-refundable, and owed once the customs declaration is accepted. The 2-EUR amount is still based on drafts that, according to German customs (zoll.de), have not been finally adopted; the mechanism itself has been announced. For a parcel with three product categories, 9 EUR of duty plus 6 EUR of handling fees can quickly stack up from November 2026.

IOSS consignments: always 3 EUR, no preference

Many Swiss shops handle EU parcels through the IOSS procedure (Import One-Stop-Shop): VAT is collected already at checkout and remitted through one EU member state. As far as customs duty is concerned, IOSS changes nothing about the payment obligation – quite the opposite:

According to the Austrian BMF, Article 2(a) of Regulation (EU) 2026/382 provides that for IOSS consignments the 3-euro duty applies across the board; customs preference is excluded. The preference code "500" must be declared in the customs declaration. If you sell via IOSS, you should therefore build the 3 EUR per goods line firmly into your margin.

For non-IOSS distance-sale consignments up to 150 EUR the flat duty applies as well – here a preference (e.g. the Switzerland–EU free trade agreement) may apply in individual cases, provided proof of origin is available. The customs service provider checks this case by case.

What this means for Swiss shops in practice

The flat duty is small – its impact is not always:

  • Cart size: on a 25-euro product, 3 EUR in duty plus clearance handling costs is a noticeable surcharge – and a classic reason for checkout abandonment if the customer only sees it upon delivery.
  • Count the item lines: mixed parcels are billed by goods category. Assortment bundling (more copies of the same category per parcel) remains advantageous in customs terms.
  • Hand-over vs. DDP: anyone offering "Delivered Duty Paid" (clearance handled by the shop) must pre-finance the 3 EUR and factor it into the price; anyone leaving payment to the customer risks delivery refusals and returns.
  • Christmas business: November and December are the first peak under the new rules – the first real test for checkout logic and communication.

Transitional rules until mid-2028

The 3-euro flat duty is expressly a time-limited transitional solution. The actual EU customs reform envisages a central EU customs data platform (Customs Data Hub) through which import duties will eventually be collected entirely digitally – no earlier than 2028. Until then, the flat-rate regime for small parcels applies; details on reference amounts and security guarantees are governed by Implementing Regulation (EU) 2015/2447 (Article 155(3)(c) UCC-IA, as amended by Regulation (EU) 2026/1200).

For Swiss sellers this means: the 3 EUR are the beginning, not the end, of regulation. Anyone who keeps their data clean today (commodity codes, values, origin) will have the advantage in 2028.

Checklist: these six points to review now

  1. IOSS registration up to date? EU identification number visible at checkout?
  2. Commodity codes (CN codes) maintained per article – so the customs declaration groups the item lines correctly?
  3. Price display: does the checkout show "duty included" or "plus import duties"? Both are legal; misleading the customer is not.
  4. Logistics contract: who handles clearance – carrier, customs broker, or the shop? Who bears the 3 EUR under DDP?
  5. Customer communication: information text on the product page and in shipment tracking.
  6. Margin: is 3 EUR per goods line calculated into the assortment – including promotional items?
  7. From November 2026: budget the additional 2-EUR Union Handling Fee per category – adjust checkout pricing before the Christmas peak.

In short

  • Since 1 July 2026: no more 150-euro customs duty exemption in the EU (Council Regulation (EU) 2026/382 of 11 February 2026).
  • 3 EUR customs duty per goods category (per item line of the customs declaration) for distance-sale consignments from third countries – including Switzerland.
  • For IOSS consignments the flat duty always applies; customs preference excluded (code 500).
  • Import VAT remains due independently of all this.
  • From 1 Nov 2026 an additional 2-EUR handling fee (Union Handling Fee) per product category is announced – amount still in draft.
  • Transitional rules until the EU customs platform (no earlier than 2028) – clean data pays off twice.
  • General information, not legal advice – for individual cases consult a customs broker or specialist lawyer.
