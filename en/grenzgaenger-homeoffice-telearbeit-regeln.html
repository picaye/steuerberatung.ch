Working from home across the border: what applies in 2026/2027

If you commute across the border and work from home, you are moving on two separate legal tracks: tax law (which country may tax your salary?) and social security law (which country are you insured in?). Most mistakes happen because rules from one track get applied to the other. State of play for 2026/2027, each point checked against the primary text:

  • France (tax): Three regimes depending on the canton of work — the 1983 agreement (eight border cantons: salary taxable only in France, Switzerland receives a 4.5 % compensation), the canton of Geneva (full Swiss source taxation, 3.5 % payment to Ain/Haute-Savoie, a 15 % threshold for telework), and the remaining cantons (ordinary treaty). Shared across all: the 40 % annual telework limit (supplementary convention in force since 24 July 2025).
  • Germany (tax): The CH-DE treaty has no percentage telework tolerance. The day principle (Art. 15) plus the limit of 60 non-return days (Art. 15a para. 2) apply.
  • Social security (all neighbouring countries): The multilateral framework agreement allows up to 49.9 % telework without switching your insurance — but only with an A1 certificate.

France: three regimes — the canton of work decides

For commuters resident in France, the tax allocation depends on where the employer sits. Three regimes, one common denominator: the 40 % annual telework limit (retroactive to remuneration paid from 1 January 2023, permanent since the supplementary convention entered into force on 24 July 2025).

  • Cantons of the 1983 agreement (BE, BL, BS, JU, NE, SO, VD, VS): the salary is taxable exclusively in France; France remits a 4.5 % compensation of the gross payroll to Switzerland. Telework up to 40 % changes nothing; beyond it the commuter status is lost.
  • Canton of Geneva (agreement of 29 January 1973): Switzerland taxes the salary fully at source; Geneva pays the departments of Ain and Haute-Savoie 3.5 % of the gross payroll annually. For the telework compensation a 15 % threshold applies: payment is due only on the share between 15 and 40 %.
  • Remaining cantons (ordinary treaty): income is taxed where the work is physically done. Telework from France counts as performed in Switzerland up to 40 % of annual working time (Telework Protocol No. 1 para. a); Switzerland remits France 40 % of the tax on the telework share.

Temporary assignments (business trips) in the state of residence or a third country count inside the 40 % — but only up to ten days per year in total (No. 3). Cross the 40 % line and the income is split under Art. 17 paras. 1–3 from the first telework day of the year: the telework share becomes taxable in the state of residence and the compensation falls away (No. 1 para. d). That is the expensive tipping effect — it reaches back over the whole year.

Germany: no percentage cap, but 60 non-return days

Contrary to what is often read: the CH-DE treaty (SR 0.672.913.62) contains no telework provision at all. The percentages quoted around are social-security rules (see below). For tax, the treaty keeps:

  • Art. 15 (day principle): employment income is taxed where the work is physically done. Home-office days in Germany are worked in your state of residence and taxed there with your worldwide income; Switzerland taxes the days worked on its territory.
  • Art. 15a (commuter privilege): Switzerland levies only 4.5 % withholding tax on the gross salary (with the residence certificate, form Gre-1). This rate does not depend on the number of home-office days but on regular return: the status is lost only if you spend more than 60 working days per calendar year not returning home for work reasons (trips with overnight stays, on-call duty).
  • Per the competent-authority agreement, regular return exists if you travel from home to the workplace and back on at least 20 % of the contracted working days.

Plain home-office: working from your German home is by definition a return to your residence — home-office days are not non-return days. Only work-related overnight stays away from home count against the 60-day budget.

Social security: 25 / 49.9 % — same for DE, FR and IT

On the social-security track one regime applies to all neighbouring countries (multilateral framework agreement under Art. 16 of Regulation (EC) 883/2004, applied by Switzerland since 1 July 2023, for Italy since 1 January 2024):

  • Below 25 % telework in the state of residence: competence stays with Switzerland under the ordinary rules anyway.
  • Between 25 and 49.9 %: Switzerland remains competent — but only if the employer requests an A1 certificate from the Swiss compensation fund via the ALPS platform (valid up to 3 years, renewable). Without the A1 you are insured in your country of residence.
  • Above 50 %: the state of residence becomes competent.

Key point: the framework agreement concerns social security only, not taxation. Exceeding the 40 % tax line with France or the 60-day line with Germany does not move you out of Swiss social insurance as long as telework stays under 49.9 % — and vice versa.

Reporting and certificate duties since 2025

  • Employer certificate (Art. 5a of the Withholding Tax Ordinance, QStV): since 1 January 2025, upon a mid-year departure the former employer must, on the employee's request, issue a certificate of telework days/rate, temporary assignments in the state of residence and third countries, and overnight stays in Switzerland (ESTV form). Needed for the correct hand-over to the new employer.
  • Automatic payroll data exchange CH-FR: the ESTV reports telework days/rate and gross remuneration of affected employees to France annually by 30 November of the following year (Art. 28ter CH-FR treaty).

Practical: checklist per year

  1. Count telework days and temporary assignments per calendar year (the employer is on the hook; keep your own record to be safe).
  2. FR relation: stay under 40 %, third/residence-state assignments under 10 days.
  3. DE relation: keep work-related overnight stays under 60, renew Gre-1 every year.
  4. Social security: from 25 % telework, have the employer apply for the A1.
  5. Changing jobs mid-year: request the Art. 5a QStV certificate.

General information, not legal or tax advice. Only the treaty texts (fedlex AS 2025 486, SR 0.672.913.62) and the BSV/ESTV guidance are authoritative. Details depend on the canton of work and the individual case — clarify borderline situations early.
