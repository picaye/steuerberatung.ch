Mortgage and taxes: interest, deductions and limits explained

The principle: imputed rental value vs. deductions

If you live in your own home, you pay tax on the imputed rental value – a notional rent that is added to your income. In return, you may deduct the costs of the property: above all mortgage interest, maintenance, and contributions to energy-saving and protective measures. The system is meant to put rented and owner-occupied property on a similar tax footing – it is not a gift.

Mortgage interest: deductible, but capped

Mortgage interest on owner-occupied residential property is private debt interest. For direct federal tax, private debt interest is deductible – but not without limits: only up to taxable investment income plus a further CHF 50'000 (Art. 33 para. 1 lit. a DBG). If you have little income from assets (interest, dividends, rent), the cap applies: not everything the bank charges you during the year ends up in the tax calculation. The cantons handle private debt interest differently – for your own tax return, the respective cantonal law is what counts.

Declaring it correctly: Schedule of Debts (Form 14)

Enter all mortgages and loans in the Schedule of Debts (Form 14): creditor, outstanding balance at year-end, interest amount. The supporting document is the interest statement from your bank – without it, the tax office will not accept the deduction. The debt itself also reduces your taxable assets; your wealth tax falls.

What you CANNOT deduct

  • Construction loan interest during the building phase: it is capital expenditure on the building, not an income deduction – recoverable later through depreciation of the construction costs.
  • Luxury expenditure: tax authorities regularly do not accept fitted items of a luxurious nature (whirlpools, special wellness fittings) as maintenance.
  • Invented rules: a “5 % interest deduction on the loan” does not exist – nor do forms “at the AHV”: the AHV is never responsible for mortgages.

Strategy: amortise or repay?

Higher mortgage rates make amortisation attractive: every repayment stage saves future interest – but it also lowers your deduction and increases your net taxable income. If you are close to the cap on private debt interest, amortising is often the better move than the (blocked) interest deduction. The imputed-rental-value-versus-deductions comparison is a matter of canton and individual case – this is where individual advice pays off more than almost anywhere else.

In short

  1. Mortgage interest is deductible – but private debt interest is capped at investment income + CHF 50'000 at the federal level (Art. 33 para. 1 lit. a DBG).
  2. Declare it in the Schedule of Debts (Form 14); supporting document = bank interest statement.
  3. Construction-phase interest is capital expenditure, not an income deduction.
  4. Check cantonal differences – especially for amortisation and the imputed rental value.
