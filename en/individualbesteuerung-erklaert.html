Individual taxation: what the Yes vote of 8 March 2026 means for you

Published on 2026-09-15 · Independent tax information for Switzerland.

Contents:
  1. What was decided?
  2. Timetable: when will the new system apply?
  3. Who benefits – and who may pay more?
  4. Not quite finished politically: The Centre party initiative

What was decided?

On 8 March 2026, Swiss voters approved individual taxation with 54.23% of votes in favour. This marks a change of system: in future, every person will be taxed individually, regardless of marital status – income and wealth will no longer be aggregated but attributed separately to each person.

The goal: to abolish the so-called marriage penalty. Today, dual-earner couples slip into a higher tax progression bracket through joint assessment and pay more tax than an unmarried couple with the same incomes. The reform removes this disadvantage – at all three levels of government (federal, cantonal, municipal).

Practical tip: Even though the headlines were big, there is no immediate need for action. The reform is not coming straight away, and large asset transfers between spouses should not be made hastily "just in case".

Timetable: when will the new system apply?

The change of system will not take effect immediately. The legislator deliberately provided for a long transition period:

  • By 1 January 2032 at the latest, individual taxation must apply – the Federal Council may set an earlier date.
  • In the meantime, the Confederation and the cantons are adapting their tax laws, tariffs and IT systems.
  • The cantons have around six years for implementation – the concrete design of tariffs and deductions at cantonal and municipal level remains a cantonal matter.

Nothing changes yet for your 2026 tax return (filed in 2027): married couples will continue to be jointly assessed.

Who benefits – and who may pay more?

The winners of the reform are clear: dual-earner couples with middle and higher incomes where both partners earn similar amounts. They benefit most because tax progression will in future be applied to two separate incomes rather than to the combined total.

The picture is less clear for single-earner couples: without the splitting advantage, their tax burden may stay the same or rise slightly – the cantons can counteract this, for example through adjusted tariffs or higher deductions. At federal level, the new law also provides for an adjustment of tariffs and an increased child deduction.

Also important: until the reform takes effect, the dual-earner deduction (50% of the lower income, CHF 8'600 to CHF 14'100) remains the instrument against the marriage penalty – forgetting it in your tax return costs you real money today. Read more in our article Saving tax.

Not quite finished politically: The Centre party initiative

One issue continues to create uncertainty: The Centre party has submitted the initiative "Yes to fair federal taxes for married couples too". It seeks to enshrine joint taxation in the Constitution – with an "alternative tax calculation": whichever is the cheaper variant of joint and separate assessment would apply in each case.

The Federal Council rejects the initiative because it contradicts the change of system approved by the people. If it goes to a vote and is accepted, a conflict of objectives would arise between the two models. Until then: individual taxation has been decided – the exact timing also depends on this initiative.

What you should do now (and what not)

  1. Do not shift assets hastily: marital property law adjustments between spouses "just in case" can have drawbacks and are rarely necessary for tax purposes.
  2. Keep using the dual-earner deduction: the current system applies until 2032 – every deduction counts.
  3. Review your pension planning: pillar 3a and pension fund buy-ins remain attractive regardless of the change of system – see Pension fund buy-in.
  4. Watch developments in your canton: how your canton adjusts tariffs and deductions determines your actual tax relief. Our guides keep you up to date.
  5. Get advice for complex cases: with substantial wealth, self-employment or an international connection, an individual calculation is worthwhile – through the free initial consultation we will match you with a vetted partner.

In short: the direction is clear, the details will follow over the coming years. Anyone who consistently uses their deductions today and recalculates their situation in 2030/2031 is doing everything right.
