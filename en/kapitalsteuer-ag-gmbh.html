Capital tax for AG and GmbH: what companies pay even in loss years

What the capital tax levies on

The capital tax is a cantonal tax on the taxable equity capital of a corporation: stock corporations (AG), limited liability companies (GmbH) and cooperatives are taxed annually on their balance-sheet equity capital (share capital plus statutory and voluntary reserves, depending on cantonal law) – regardless of whether the company makes a profit. A loss year does not exempt you; the tax is still due.

At federal level there is no capital tax – the direct federal tax knows only the profit tax (8.5 %). Pure profit-tax cantons are rare: the majority of cantons levy a capital tax, though in very different designs. The canton of Uri, for example, waives it entirely at cantonal level (FTA publication «Steuersatz und Steuerfuss», as of 1.1.2026), while Uri's communes levy proportional rates between 0.01 and 4 per mille.

Rates and tax multipliers: a tour

The statutory rates move in the per-mille range. Example Aargau: 0.75 per mille of taxable equity capital (§ 86 para. 1 of the Aargau Tax Act); at a multiplier of 115 % (2026) that works out to around CHF 1'725 capital tax per year for a company with CHF 2 million of equity capital. The canton of Lucerne applies a fixed capital tax of only 0.01 per mille to the share of equity capital attributable to participations, patents and comparable rights as well as intra-group receivables – the typical holding arrangement (FTA, op. cit.).

The burden is not capped everywhere: the canton of Vaud provides by law that cantonal and communal taxes together may not exceed 7 per mille of taxable capital (and 30 % of taxable net profit). The majority of cantons, by contrast, provide no overall burden cap for legal entities.

Tax credit: the double burden has a damper

Many cantons soften the combination of profit tax and capital tax by crediting the profit tax against the capital tax – expressly so in Aargau (§ 86 para. 4 of the Aargau Tax Act). In practice this means: in profitable years the capital tax has often already been «absorbed»; the company effectively pays it on top mainly when the capital tax exceeds the profit tax – i.e. in weak and loss years.

Minimum taxes remain: in Aargau, corporations pay a minimum tax of CHF 500 and cooperatives CHF 100 (in each case the simple 100 % cantonal tax, § 88 of the Aargau Tax Act) – even with zero profit; newly founded companies are exempt from the minimum tax for their first five years. Associations and foundations become subject to the capital tax only from CHF 50'000 of equity capital (§ 86 para. 3 of the Aargau Tax Act).

Who really feels the burden?

The capital tax is a wealth tax on company money. Three constellations hurt:

  • Cash-rich, profit-poor companies: saved reserves increase the equity capital and thus the tax, without any profit having flowed – the tax becomes a pure liquidity burden.
  • Loss years: profit tax zero, capital tax full – the credit misses its target.
  • Reorganisation cases: before capital reductions, restructurings and liquidations, the capital tax belongs in the modelling: until the company is struck off the tax is owed annually; a staged capital reduction can lower it, but depending on the construction it triggers questions on repayment of the issuance stamp tax or withholding tax.

A cantonal location factor – and a permanent political debate

The capital tax is a genuine location criterion: between «no cantonal capital tax» (Uri) and several per mille including the communal share lie worlds – added to the profit tax, it burdens capital-intensive models over years. Individual cantons have cut rates or created special rules for holding substance; the political debate on total abolition regularly returns at cantonal level.

For practice this means: whoever founds, holds, restructures or liquidates an AG or GmbH checks the domicile canton's capital tax anew every year – rates, multipliers and credit models change more often than the profit tax.

Checklist for AG/GmbH shareholders

  1. Keep a standing register: taxable equity capital per year incl. changes in reserves.
  2. Compare cantons: know the rate, multiplier, credit model and minimum tax of the domicile canton (FTA «Steuersatz und Steuerfuss» as starting point).
  3. Plan for loss years: capital tax + minimum tax flow regardless of the result.
  4. Reserve policy: retention decisions raise the capital tax base – compute dividend vs. reserve on the total effect (incl. 35 % withholding tax).
  5. Model restructurings: capital reduction, partial liquidation, liquidation – capital tax savings vs. issuance stamp tax and withholding tax consequences.
  6. Revalidate annually: multipliers are often changed.

In short

  • Capital tax = cantonal annual tax on the equity capital of AG/GmbH/cooperatives – also in loss years.
  • The federal tax knows no capital tax; cantonal rates lie in the per-mille range (example Aargau 0.75 ‰; Uri at cantonal level: none; Vaud cap 7 ‰).
  • The profit tax is usually credited – effectively you pay capital tax mainly in weak results; minimum taxes remain (Aargau: CHF 500).
  • In capital reductions and liquidations it belongs in the overall modelling.
  • General information, not individual advice – engage a fiduciary or tax expert.
