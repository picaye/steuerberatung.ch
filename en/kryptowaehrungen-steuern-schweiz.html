Bitcoin & Crypto on Your Tax Return: How to Declare It Correctly

The basic rule: wealth yes, gains usually no

Switzerland takes a straightforward approach to crypto held as private assets: capital gains from the sale of private assets are tax-free (Art. 16 para. 3 FATA/DBG). If you buy Bitcoin, hold it and sell at a profit, you generally pay no income tax on the gain – unlike in many other countries.

However, your crypto holdings are subject to wealth tax. You must therefore declare them as of 31 December – with quantity and value. Valuation is based on the official year-end tax rates published by the Federal Tax Administration (FTA), which issues an annual rate list. For coins without an FTA rate, the market value applies (e.g. the price on the largest trading platform). Examples: Bitcoin (BTC) is included in the FTA rate list and valued at the official year-end tax rate; newer coins such as Kaspa (KAS) are often not yet listed — declare them at the documented market value on the largest trading platform.

Practical tip: enter your crypto in the securities schedule under «Miscellaneous» – with designation, quantity and rate value. A screenshot of your exchange balance as of 31.12 is a useful attachment in case the tax office asks.

Worked example: valuing 2 BTC and 10,000 KAS

Anna holds the following as of 31.12.2025 (2025 tax year): 2 Bitcoin (BTC) in a hardware wallet and 10,000 Kaspa (KAS) on a trading platform. She is a long-term holder and works without leverage – this is how she values the holdings at the reference date:

  • Bitcoin – with an FTA tax rate: The FTA rate list sets the tax value per 1 BTC as of 31.12.2025 at CHF 69'571.99 → 2 × 69'571.99 = CHF 139'143.98.
  • Kaspa – without an FTA tax rate: Kaspa is not on the FTA rate list as of 31.12.2025. Anna documents the rate on the largest trading platform at the reference date – in this example CHF 0.035 per KAS → 10,000 × 0.035 = CHF 350.

Total: Anna enters CHF 139'493.98 in the securities schedule – the amount increases her taxable wealth. How much wealth tax results depends on the canton and commune – the tax calculator computes it online.

And what remains tax-free: if Anna sells her 2 BTC in 2026 at a profit and is still classified as a private investor (see the next section), the gain is not taxable. From the following 31.12., the sale proceeds simply sit in the bank account – the same value, a different place.

Practical tip: keep the FTA rate list and the screenshot of your exchange balance as of 31.12.2025 together with the completed securities schedule – that way you can substantiate every position at any time.

When gains are taxable after all: professional trading

The tax exemption applies only to private asset management. Anyone classified as a professional trader pays income tax on gains – plus AHV (social security) contributions. The FTA assesses this under Circular 36 using several criteria; typical indicators of private classification are:

  • A holding period of at least 6 months
  • Annual transaction volume below 5 times the portfolio value at the start of the year
  • Realised gains below 50% of taxable income
  • No debt financing (no buying on credit/leverage)
  • No or only minimal derivatives and margin trading

An overall assessment applies – no single criterion is decisive. Day traders using leverage and borrowed capital quickly fall into the professional-trading category; long-term holders generally remain private.

Staking, mining, airdrops: that is income

Not everything is tax-free. The following in particular are taxable as income at the time of receipt:

  • Staking and lending rewards – at market value when received
  • Mining – where the activity is substantial, as self-employment (including AHV and, where applicable, VAT)
  • Airdrops and newly allocated tokens from forks – at the value when received, provided they are available to you
  • Interest on crypto accounts (earn products, stablecoin lending)

Important: these proceeds also increase your acquisition costs later on – declaring inflows correctly avoids double taxation when you sell.

Practical tip: export the transaction history (CSV) from your exchanges and wallets once a year. Doing this regularly saves you hours in March – and lets you document every inflow.

CARF from 2027: automatic data exchange is coming

Under the OECD's Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework (CARF), crypto data will in future be exchanged automatically between tax authorities – similar to bank accounts today under AEOI/CRS. For Switzerland: implementation comes at the earliest on 1 January 2027, with the first automatic exchange planned for 2028, covering relevant transactions from 2027.

There are thus no CARF reporting obligations in Switzerland for 2026 yet. However: foreign exchanges (e.g. Binance, Kraken) already report to their local authorities today, which can pass the data on. And your obligation to declare exists regardless – CARF changes nothing about the fact that you must declare your crypto.

Anyone who declares correctly has nothing to fear. If you have so far not declared crypto held on foreign platforms, consider a voluntary disclosure (penalty-free the first time per person; back taxes for up to 10 years).

Checklist for your crypto declaration

In short – this is what you need for your tax return:

  • Holdings of all wallets and exchanges as of 31.12 (quantity per coin)
  • FTA year-end tax rates or documented market values for the valuation
  • Evidence of holding periods for larger sales (professional-trading check)
  • A statement of all staking, lending, mining and airdrop proceeds with dates of receipt
  • In case of losses: supporting documents – losses on private assets are generally not deductible (except for professional traders or special cases depending on the canton)
