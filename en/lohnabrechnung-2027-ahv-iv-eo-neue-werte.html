What the Federal Council decided on 2 October 2026

At its meeting of 2 October 2026, the Federal Council raised AHV/IV pensions by approximately 1.59 per cent with effect from 1 January 2027 (mixed price/wage index; exact adjustment factor 1.587 %, new pension index 232.7 points). The minimum pension rises from CHF 1,260 to CHF 1,280, the maximum pension from CHF 2,520 to CHF 2,560 per month.

For pensioners that is the end of the story — the increase is automatic (details in our article AHV pensions 2027). For employers, payroll offices and fiduciaries, however, the 2 October decision is only the starting point: the same adjustment ordinance (AS 2026 542, published 14 Oct 2026, in force 1 Jan 2027) also raises the contribution-side values — minimum contributions, the degressive scale for the self-employed and the wealth thresholds for non-employed persons. In parallel, the Federal Council adjusted the occupational pension thresholds (AS 2026 547: entry threshold CHF 23,040, coordination deduction CHF 26,880, upper limit CHF 92,160).

The contribution rates themselves (AHV/IV/EO total 10.6 % of AHV salary, split evenly between employer and employee) remain unchanged — what changes are the framework values. They must be loaded into payroll software before the January 2027 payroll run; anyone processing the December run in advance already needs them.

Payroll from January 2027: rates stay, caps move

The good news for payroll: nothing changes in the percentages. Employers and employees continue to pay 5.3 per cent each in AHV/IV/EO (AHV 4.35 % + IV 0.7 % + EO 0.25 %) of AHV salary — 10.6 per cent in total. As of today the ALV also stays at 1.1 per cent per side up to insured earnings of CHF 148,200 (the solidarity surcharge has been abolished since 2023); any change to ALV rates would be a separate Federal Council decision, usually taken in late autumn.

What moves is the cap: contributions on salaries above the top table bracket are levied as one tenth of the relevant income (Art. 23 para. 1 AHVG), up to ten times the table limit — i.e. up to CHF 307,200 of annual income (2026: CHF 302,400); amounts above that are refunded (Art. 23 para. 2 AHVG). Specifically:

  • Anyone earning exactly at the cap in 2027 pays 5.3 % of CHF 307,200 = CHF 16,281.60 per year per side (2026: CHF 16,027.20) — an increase of CHF 254.40 per side, CHF 508.80 in total.
  • Salaries between CHF 302,400 and CHF 307,200 become fully contribution-liable for the first time in 2027 — worth a look in your salary budgeting.

For the EO the daily-allowance ceilings also stay put: maximum total allowance CHF 275 per day, adoption allowance CHF 220 per day (Art. 7 of the adjustment ordinance).

Practical tip: run your 2027 labour-cost scenarios through our calculator before budgeting — especially for salaries near the cap and for part-time employees with several jobs (each employment relationship has its own ALV cap).

Since the rates do not change in 2027, this calculation is identical for 2026 and 2027 — the differences lie exclusively in the caps and minimum contributions (see table above). That is why the January switch-over is uneventful for standard salaries: load the software parameters in time and payroll continues without manual work.

Self-employed persons and board members: new minimum contribution, new scale

For owner-managed companies, sole proprietorships and officers paid by their own business, the fixed values change:

  • The minimum contribution for self-employed and non-employed persons rises from CHF 530 to CHF 541 per year (AHV 444 + IV 71 + EO 26), i.e. CHF 45.10 per month.
  • The degressive contribution scale now applies to income between CHF 10,300 and CHF 61,500 (previously 10,100–60,500); the lowest scale rate is 5.371 %, and the maximum rate of 10 per cent applies from CHF 61,500.
  • The allowance at reference age (contributions only on income above CHF 16,800) remains unchanged.
  • The minimum contribution for voluntary AHV/IV (Swiss nationals abroad) rises from CHF 1,010 to CHF 1,030.

The new contribution tables for self-employed and non-employed persons (doc. 318.114, version 14) were already published by the FSIO on 7 October 2026, valid from 1 Jan 2027. Background and calculation examples for owner-managers: AHV minimum contribution for the self-employed.

Board fees remain employment income: the company deducts the standard 5.3 % + 5.3 % AHV/IV/EO (plus ALV) regardless of amount. If you combine fees and dividends, keep the overall remuneration at arm's length — on current case law see our article on Federal Supreme Court ruling 9C_532/2025 (dividends instead of salary).

Mini-jobs and casual staff: the CHF 2,500 threshold stays

The threshold for minor earnings is not touched by the adjustment ordinance: if you pay a casual worker no more than CHF 2,500 per calendar year and employer, you generally account for no AHV/IV/EO/ALV contributions on it — unless the insured person expressly requests it (simplified settlement procedure; info sheet 2.04). The amount had only been raised from CHF 2,300 to CHF 2,500 with effect from 1 Jan 2025 and stays unchanged for 2027.

Take care in December and January with seasonal and casual staff: the CHF 2,500 is a threshold, not an allowance. Exceed it by one franc and the entire salary becomes contribution-liable — all payments from the same employer within the calendar year count together, including ancillary benefits and benefits in kind. Non-employed persons: new limits for the minimum and maximum contribution

The contribution duty of non-employed persons (assessed on wealth plus 20 times annual pension income) is also updated as of 1 Jan 2027:

  • Minimum contribution: CHF 541 per year (previously 530) — due where wealth + 20 × pension income is below CHF 360,000 (previously 350,000).
  • Maximum contribution: CHF 27,050 per year (50 × minimum; previously 26,500) — reached at CHF 9,160,000 or more (previously 8,950,000); the middle threshold rises from CHF 1,750,000 to CHF 1,760,000.
  • For married couples, the wealth and pension income of both spouses is added together and then halved.

This matters to employers in cases of unpaid leave, early retirement and accompanying partners of expatriate staff: anyone who leaves gainful employment — or works less than half the usual workload — may become contribution-liable as a non-employed person, and the compensation fund typically follows up the next year. The new tables (pp. 25–30 of doc. 318.114) apply from 1 Jan 2027.

Payroll timetable: from the December run to 31 January

The switch-over is mandatory but plannable. The schedule until end of January 2027:

  1. November/December 2026: install the Q4 payroll software update (AHV/IV/EO parameters 2027, new caps) and import the compensation funds' new tables for employees as soon as they appear — usually in November/December.
  2. December payroll: December salaries still run on the 2026 values; the 2027 parameters take effect with the first January salary. If you already process January advance payments in December, you need the new values now.
  3. Q1 2027: instalment payments (Akontobeiträge) to the compensation fund are reassessed on the new salary total — after major salary rounds or headcount cuts, have the instalment level reviewed proactively.
  4. Pension fund: entry threshold CHF 23,040, coordination deduction CHF 26,880, upper limit CHF 92,160 (AS 2026 547). Check whether part-time staff fall below or above the new entry threshold in 2027 and whether your fund's regulations adopt the values as of 1 Jan 2027.
  5. By end of January 2027: the annual salary declaration (report of 2026 contribution-relevant salaries) to the compensation fund — still on the old values. Missing the deadline is costly: the fund then assesses at its discretion.
  6. Check separately (cantonal): family allowance rates (FAK) and 2027 withholding tax tariffs — both change independently of the AHV indexation.
  7. Inform employees: higher pension entitlements only arise from uninterrupted contributions. An individual account statement reveals gaps: checking and closing AHV contribution gaps.

Status (21 Oct 2026): all values marked "decided" above are published in the Official Compilation and therefore final. Only the compensation funds' implementing tables for employees and any separate decision on 2027 ALV rates remain open — both usually known by early December.

In short

  • Federal Council, 2 Oct 2026: AHV/IV pensions +1.59 % from 1 Jan 2027; minimum pension CHF 1,280, maximum CHF 2,560 per month (AS 2026 542).
  • Contribution rates for employees and employers stay at 5.3 % each for AHV/IV/EO (plus ALV 1.1 % up to CHF 148,200) — the cap on contribution-relevant annual income rises to CHF 307,200.
  • Minimum contribution self-employed/non-employed: CHF 530 → 541; scale now CHF 10,300–61,500; maximum contribution non-employed CHF 27,050 (wealth thresholds CHF 360,000/9,160,000).
  • BVG from 1 Jan 2027: entry threshold CHF 23,040, coordination deduction CHF 26,880, upper limit CHF 92,160 (AS 2026 547).
  • Unchanged: CHF 2,500 bagatelle threshold, EO daily allowances (CHF 275/220), reference-age allowance CHF 16,800; 2027 ALV rates decided separately (unchanged as of today).
  • Payroll software update in December; first affected run: January 2027; 2026 annual declaration due by end of January 2027.
