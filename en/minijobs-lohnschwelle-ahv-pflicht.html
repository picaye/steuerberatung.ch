Mini-jobs and seasonal staff: when payroll contributions and reporting duties are triggered

The CHF 2'500 limit: minor wages

If you pay an assistant at most CHF 2'500 per calendar year, you generally do not have to settle contributions to the AHV (old-age and survivors' insurance), IV (disability insurance), EO (income loss compensation scheme) or ALV (unemployment insurance) — unless the insured person explicitly requests them. This is the exemption for minor remuneration (factsheet 2.04 of the AHV compensation offices). The amount was raised from CHF 2'300 to CHF 2'500 as of 1 January 2025.

Crucially: the limit applies per employer and per calendar year. All remuneration an employer pays to the same person during the current year — including additional benefits, tips and benefits in kind — must be added together.

A threshold, not an allowance — the classic mistake

If CHF 2'500 is exceeded by even one franc, the entire wage becomes contribution-liable — not just the excess amount. The compensation offices deliberately speak of a limit, not a deduction. If a third assignment in December pushes you over the threshold, contributions on the annual wage are owed retroactively — including default interest.

Contributions follow the full rate: 5.3 % AHV/IV/EO plus 1.1 % ALV per side (the employer pays half; the total is 12.8 %), plus administration costs and family allowances via the cantonal compensation office.

Exceptions: private households, culture and media

For two areas the exemption does not apply — contributions are due from the first franc:

  • Private households (domestic work: cleaning, gardening, childcare, housekeeping): contributions are due in every case (factsheet 2.06). Exception for allowance-type jobs (Sackgeldjobs): in private households, wages up to CHF 750 per year per employer are free of contributions as long as the employed person has not yet reached 31 December following their 25th birthday (as of 2026: birth years 2001 and later; there is no lower age limit).
  • Culture and media workers: wages from dance and theatre productions, audio and image producers, radio and television, and artistic training institutions are always subject to contributions. From 1 January 2026 the Federal Council extended contribution liability via an amendment to the AHV Ordinance (Art. 34d para. 2 let. b AHVO, AS 2025 226) to further sectors — now including, among others, design, museums, media and choirs (information from the AHV compensation office of Solothurn).

Who benefits from demanding contributions — and who is liable

Even below CHF 2'500, the insured person can demand that contributions be paid. This is worthwhile especially for students and young professionals: the contributions fill gaps in the individual AHV account and can later be credited against the non-earning contribution of married persons.

Conversely: if the employee accepts the wage in full without deduction, the employer can no longer reclaim the employee's share retroactively. And an employer who does not pay contributions at all, or pays them late, is liable under Art. 52 AHVG for the entire damage — bearing even the employee's share, with no right of recourse.

Reporting duties and withholding tax on short-term wages

Contribution exemption does not mean anonymity: even minimally employed persons must be reported to the compensation office as soon as contributions are owed or demanded. For foreigners without residence in Switzerland (cross-border commuters, short-term residents), withholding tax applies from the first franc — the AHV exemption does not protect against that. The salary statement must also be issued for mini-jobs.

Checklist for the season start (November to January)

  1. Track the annual wage per person and employer — including all additional benefits; check the cumulative total before every new assignment.
  2. Check the sector: private household, culture, design, media, museum, choir? Then contributions are due from the first franc.
  3. Note the assistant's age (allowance-type-job rule up to 25 years in private households).
  4. Document any demand for contributions if the person wants them paid.
  5. Plan for the withholding-tax duty from the first franc when the employee resides abroad.
  6. Issue and retain the salary statement even for mini-jobs (10 years).

In short

  • Up to CHF 2'500 in wages per person and employer per calendar year: no AHV/IV/EO/ALV contributions — unless the person requests them (factsheet 2.04; limit in force since 1.1.2025).
  • Exceeding the limit = contributions on the entire wage (threshold, not allowance) — retroactive claim including default interest.
  • Private households and the culture/media sector (extended from 2026 to design, museums, media, choirs): contributions from the first franc.
  • The employer is liable under Art. 52 AHVG for employee shares that were not deducted.
  • Withholding tax on foreign residence applies regardless of the AHV limit.
  • General information, not individual advice — questions are clarified with the responsible compensation office.
