VAT from CHF 100'000: When You Become Liable for Value Added Tax

The threshold: CHF 100'000 – worldwide

You become liable for value added tax (VAT) once your business generates CHF 100'000 or more a year from supplies that are not excluded from tax (Art. 10 para. 2 of the Value Added Tax Act, VATA). Important: what counts is turnover at home and abroad, not just your Swiss turnover. Excluded supplies (for example letting residential property, insurance and banking transactions, certain educational services) are not counted.

Anyone who stays below the limit over a year is exempt from VAT liability – with an exception for associations: non-profit, volunteer-run sports and cultural clubs and charitable organisations have an increased limit of CHF 250'000 (Art. 10 para. 2 lit. c VATA). The relevant period is always one year; anyone who can foresee exceeding the limit in the next twelve months becomes liable to VAT beforehand.

Registration: on your own initiative, within 30 days

As soon as you exceed the limit (or can foresee exceeding it), you must register in writing with the Federal Tax Administration (FTA) on your own initiative within 30 days of the start of tax liability (Art. 66 para. 1 VATA). Nobody will write to you first. Registration runs through the FTA's online questionnaire; you will be assigned a non-transferable VAT number. Room for confusion: «Form 25» concerns refunds of withholding tax – for VAT, the FTA's online form is the correct route.

Conversely: anyone who stays below the limit can voluntarily waive the exemption and register (Art. 11 and 14 VATA) – thanks to the input tax deduction, this pays off above all when you have high investments. Warning: anyone who shows VAT on an invoice without being entitled to do so still owes the amount (Art. 27 VATA).

The rates in 2026

The rates are: standard rate 8.1 per cent, reduced rate 2.6 per cent (Art. 25 VATA). The reduced rate applies, among other things, to foodstuffs within the meaning of the Foodstuffs Act (excluding alcoholic beverages), water in pipes, medicines, books and press products. Watch out – a widespread misconception: hospitality services are taxed at the standard rate – restaurant service and consumption on the premises are not reduced, even though the food itself would be. Anyone calculating «catering at 2.6 %» learned it wrong.

Settling your account: quarterly, semi-annually or at balance tax rates (Saldosteuersätze)

The standard is quarterly settlement via the FTA's VAT portal. Small businesses (a low tax amount per year) may settle semi-annually or annually; many SMEs work with the balance tax rates (Saldosteuersätze) – flat industry-specific rates that cut accounting effort, because the input tax does not have to be substantiated individually.

Side job, club, platform: when you are a business all the same

A business is anyone who pursues, on their own account, an activity aimed at generating sustainable income (Art. 10 para. 1bis VATA) – also alongside a main job, and also with a 20 per cent workload on a platform. Ongoing consulting assignments, shop sales or letting with infrastructure can trigger tax liability before you notice it. Insignificant remuneration and genuinely one-off transactions do not.

In short

  1. Liability from CHF 100'000 of worldwide turnover from supplies that are not excluded (Art. 10 para. 2 VATA); sports and cultural clubs and charitable institutions: CHF 250'000.
  2. Registration: within 30 days, on your own initiative, FTA online questionnaire – not Form 25.
  3. 2026 rates: standard 8.1 %, reduced 2.6 %; hospitality = standard rate.
  4. Voluntary registration brings the input tax deduction; VAT shown without entitlement is owed (Art. 27).
