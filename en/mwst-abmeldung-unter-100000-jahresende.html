Turnover below CHF 100,000: check VAT de-registration before the 31.12. period end

When de-registration is possible – and why the threshold alone is not enough

Deletion from the VAT register requires two tests, not one. First, the relevant turnover from taxable supplies in Switzerland and abroad may no longer reach CHF 100,000; non-profit, voluntarily run sports and cultural clubs and charitable institutions have the higher threshold of CHF 250,000 (Art. 10 para. 2 VAT Act). Second, it must be expected that the threshold will also not be reached in the following tax period. Only when both apply is there a right to the exemption (ESTV, VAT liability; MWST-Info 02).

Worked example: a graphic designer (sole proprietorship, effective method) still generated CHF 128,000 of taxable turnover in 2025 and was therefore VAT-liable. In 2026 the relevant turnover drops to CHF 92,000 net, and because a major contract has been lost no recovery is expected in 2027 either. Deletion is therefore possible at the earliest at the end of the tax period in which the threshold was first missed – for a calendar-year period, i.e. 31 December 2026.

The de-registration is filed in time if it reaches the ESTV within 60 days after the end of the tax period (Art. 14 para. 5 VAT Act). For the 31.12.2026 period end that makes the deadline 1 March 2027. Strategically, though, the decision is made now in November/December – because it depends on how the final quarter and the order backlog carry into the new year.

Practical tip: Test the threshold already on the basis of January to September when December is traditionally not your biggest phase. That buys you weeks for the final settlement – instead of a rushed decision in January.

Why doing nothing turns into voluntary tax liability – the waiver trap

The single most important sentence is in Art. 14 para. 5 VAT Act: «Failure to de-register is deemed a waiver of the exemption from tax liability.» Anyone who drops below the threshold but does not actively de-register thus legally decides to remain taxable. The voluntary liability takes effect from the start of the following tax period – the company stays registered, must keep showing VAT on invoices and filing settlements periodically.

The binding effect must not be ignored: a waiver of the exemption must be maintained for at least one tax period (Art. 11 para. 2 VAT Act). Someone who notices the mistake can neither reverse the deletion immediately nor revoke a waiver declared in the middle of a period – they are bound until the period expires.

For B2C customers this is doubly painful: showing VAT makes prices higher for end consumers without any benefit on their side – unlike corporate customers, who use the input-tax deduction. The waiver trap is therefore less a legal footnote than a direct question of pricing and administration.

Practical tip: Put the de-registration date (for a calendar period: 1 March of the following year) in your calendar now – not only in the new year once the deadline is already running.

What de-registration costs: input-tax correction and self-consumption on the final settlement

De-registration is not free. While the company was taxable it deducted input tax on purchases – vehicles, machines, tools, IT and stock. When tax liability lapses, the prerequisite for that deduction lapses retroactively: anything still within your disposal when the liability ends is treated as self-consumption (Art. 31 para. 2 lit. d VAT Act) and the input tax previously deducted must be repaid.

Movable assets are written down by one fifth per elapsed year (a correction over 5 years), immovable assets by one twentieth (20 years); unused stock, however, is corrected in full. The correction belongs in the settlement of the last tax period.

Anyone settling at a flat rate (Saldosteuersatz) is largely spared this correction: self-consumption is already accounted for in the flat rate, so only turnover and work in progress up to the deletion date are settled at your own rate. Effective-method taxpayers declare the repayment in the last ordinary settlement.

Practical tip: Compile the schedule of assets with deducted input tax (purchase date, price, VAT) before you trigger the application – this avoids a corrective settlement that can additionally trigger default interest.

Do the math: de-register or stay voluntarily taxable?

Below the threshold, exemption is a right, not a duty. That is exactly why the decision deserves a calculation. The trade-off turns on two questions: how large is your input tax on your own purchases (which you lose on deletion), and who are your customers (for whom VAT on an invoice helps or hurts)?

SituationWorthwhile pathReason
Predominantly B2C, low own purchasesDe-registerNo price disadvantage, less administration
Predominantly B2B (corporate clients)Stay voluntaryClients want the input-tax deduction
Large investments plannedStay voluntaryInput tax on assets is deductible
Stable low margin, no assetsDe-registerWaives quarterly settlements

Guiding calculation: if the graphic designer stays taxable, she charges clients VAT of CHF 7,452 on CHF 92,000 of output and claims input tax of CHF 648 on her own purchases of CHF 8,000 – the balance flows to the ESTV. If she de-registers, both disappear: no input-tax deduction, but no more VAT on invoices either. For a pure end-customer business, de-registering is usually the clear advantage.

Practical tip: When weighing it up, also consider the radio/TV reception fee (Serafe), which is based on total turnover, and any contract clauses that presuppose showing VAT.

Procedure: de-registration in the new ESTV portal – and what to do afterwards

Since 9 November 2026, VAT de-registration runs through the new ESTV portal. The application is under «Neue Geschäfte» (new transactions); the old website form is no longer available. Keep your VAT number, the turnover calculation and the desired deletion date ready, and save both the submission confirmation and the later decision with the deletion date.

Three things must be adjusted after deletion. First, new invoices may no longer show VAT – update templates and recurring invoices; the UID remains as company ID but without continuing VAT registration. Second, VAT de-registration does not cancel the commercial register or the compensation fund. Third, purchaser tax (Bezugsteuer) remains: anyone importing services from abroad (SaaS, consulting, advertising) above CHF 10,000 a year owes the tax even without VAT registration (Art. 45 para. 2 lit. b VAT Act).

Practical tip: Keep period turnover and the self-consumption correction separate in the last settlement – and submit the final settlement by the 1 March deadline even if the deletion itself only becomes legally effective later.

Quick checklist: file the VAT de-registration on time

  • Test the threshold: Is the relevant turnover (net, in and abroad, taxable supplies) below CHF 100,000 – and expected to stay so in the following period?
  • Make the decision: Use the exemption and de-register – or stay voluntarily taxable (e.g. for B2B clients or planned investments).
  • Keep the deadline: File the de-registration within 60 days after the period end – for 31.12.2026 by 1 March 2027. Non-de-registration counts as a waiver.
  • Final settlement: Declare the self-consumption correction on retained assets and stock (residual-value method).
  • Follow-up: Invoice templates without VAT, purchaser tax on imported services, commercial register and compensation fund checked separately.

Whether sole proprietorship, start-up in a down year or SME carving out a unit – de-registration is a one-off but consequential step in the year-end close. Check the deadline and the final settlement before 31 December; for assets, real estate or uncertainty about the waiver binding, ask a verified professional first.
