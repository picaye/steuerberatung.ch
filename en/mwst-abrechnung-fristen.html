The 60-day rule: filing and payment run in parallel

The key VAT deadline: within 60 days after the end of each settlement period the return must be filed unprompted with the Federal Tax Administration (FTA) — and the tax due for that period must be paid within the same window (Art. 71 para. 1 and Art. 86 para. 1 VAT Act).

Late payment triggers default interest without any reminder (Art. 87 para. 1). The other way round favours the taxpayer: if the FTA does not refund a credit surplus within 60 days of receiving the return, it owes compensation interest from day 61 (Art. 88 para. 4).

Practical tip: these dates fall on the same days every year (table below). Put the settlement periods in your calendar as recurring events.

Which settlement period applies to your company

The law provides a tiered system (Art. 35 VAT Act):

  • Quarterly settlement — the default for effective (itemised) accounting.
  • Semi-annual settlement — for the flat-rate (balance) tax method.
  • Annual settlement — on request, if taxable turnover is at most CHF 5,005,000 a year.
  • Monthly settlement — on request for regular input-tax surpluses; refunds then arrive faster.

Two switching rules are often missed: changes only take effect at the start of a tax period, and an annual settlement must be kept for at least one full period. Anyone leaving the annual method for monthly, quarterly or semi-annual returns can come back earliest after three tax periods. Companies that neglect filing or payment duties can have the annual permission refused or revoked (Art. 35a).

When the tax liability ends, the 60-day clock starts at that moment (Art. 71 para. 2) — the final return does not automatically land at calendar year-end.

Annual settlement: instalments replace quarterly returns

Annual filers do not wait a year to pay: the FTA levies provisional tax in instalments (Art. 86a). Under effective accounting and the flat-rate tax, each instalment is one quarter of the previous period's tax claim, due on the 150th, 240th and 330th day of the current tax period; under the balance tax method, one half is due on day 240. Instalments are credited against the annual return. They can be adjusted on request; late or incomplete instalments accrue default interest without reminder (Art. 87 para. 1bis).

Worked example (fictitious figures): effective settlement, previous period's tax claim CHF 12,000. The FTA bills three instalments of CHF 4,000 — due on day 150, 240 and 330 of the current calendar year, in practice end of May, end of August and end of November. The annual return itself is due by 28/29 February of the following year; instalments are credited.

Illustrative example without guarantee; the assessed tax claim of each period is decisive.

Deadline extension: free of charge — but only in the FTA portal

The FTA grants free extensions for filing — exclusively through the «Settle VAT» service in the ePortal. Paper or e-mail requests cannot be approved, because the electronic procedure is mandatory (Art. 123 of the VAT Ordinance). Businesses working with the FTA's «MWST-Abrechnung pro» software extend the deadline directly in the overview.

The distinction matters: an extension covers filing only — not payment. Money arriving after the ordinary 60-day date accrues default interest (Art. 87 para. 1) even with an approved extension. Pragmatic fix: pay an estimated amount before the due date and file the return afterwards.

Correcting errors: the 180-day rule

Errors found while preparing the annual accounts must be corrected at the latest in the return covering the period into which the 180th day after the close of the business year falls (Art. 72 para. 1). Mistakes in older tax periods must be corrected retrospectively as long as those claims are neither final nor time-barred (para. 2).

Correcting before the authority objects avoids sanctions: penalty provisions on evasion only bite once the Art. 72 para. 1 correction window has expired uncorrected.

In short

  • 60 days after each period: file the return and pay — unprompted (Art. 71 para. 1, Art. 86 para. 1).
  • Quarterly is the default; balance tax: semi-annual; on request annual (≤ CHF 5,005,000 turnover) or monthly with input-tax surpluses (Art. 35).
  • Annual settlement runs on instalments at day 150/240/330, or half at day 240 (Art. 86a).
  • Extensions are free, portal-only — and cover filing, not payment (Art. 123 VAT Ordinance, Art. 87).
  • Year-end corrections: by the 180th day after the close of the business year (Art. 72 para. 1).
