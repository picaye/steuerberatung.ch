VAT at 0 % on Exports: These Are the Export Proofs the FTA Accepts

0 % Tax Is a Question of Proof

Supplies abroad are exempt from VAT, or taxable at the rate of 0 % (Art. 6 para. 2 let. a VAT Act). But: the exemption is not a matter of course – it is a question of proof. The FTA proceeds from the principle that the taxable supplier must prove the export. If the proof fails, the supply is reassessed as a domestic transaction – at the current domestic rate of 8.1 % – even though the foreign customer paid the net amount.

This is precisely the classic finding in VAT audits: sale booked, tax declared at 0 %, but no valid proof of export on file. The reclaim hits the margin in full.

Passar Has Replaced e-dec Export (Since 1 January 2026)

For the proof of export, 2026 brought a fundamental change: the Federal Office for Customs and Border Security (FOCBS) took the old e-dec Export system out of service on 31 December 2025. Since 1 January 2026, export declarations run exclusively through Passar (www.passar.admin.ch). For exporters, this has practical consequences:

  • The electronic export assessment decision (eVV Export) is the central proof of export – it must be actively retrieved from Passar, manually via the "Chartera Output" tool or automated via customs software, and then archived electronically.
  • The decision is legally valid as a PDF only with the embedded XML part – a screenshot or a plain printout is not sufficient.
  • Extended transitional periods apply to tobacco products; the Form. 11.44 (customs-stamped duplicate of the customs declaration for raw tobacco and tobacco products) remains a valid form of proof.
  • e-dec Web can temporarily continue to be used; its definitive replacement will be communicated separately.

These Are the Documents the FTA Accepts

The FOCBS lists the suitable proofs of export, in principle exhaustively:

  1. Electronic export assessment decision (eVV Export) with digital signature – the standard case for commercial exports (from Passar since 2026).
  2. Customs-stamped duplicate of the customs declaration for raw tobacco and tobacco products (Form. 11.44).
  3. Customs-confirmed "export document in travel traffic" – the document for retail shops that sell goods to travellers (tax-free procedure; details from the FTA under "VAT-Refund: Rückerstattung an Unternehmen im Ausland").
  4. Customs documents on properly completed procedures – e.g. when an item is exported after temporary use in the country and assessed abroad.

If the export cannot be proven with any of these documents, the catch-all rule applies: the supplies must be taxed domestically.

Travel Traffic: The Document at the Border Crossing

When a retail shop sells to tourists, the export document in travel traffic replaces the commercial customs declaration: the goods are declared at the customs office on exit and stamped or confirmed electronically (export declaration). Without this document the supply counts as domestic turnover – any refund of the tax paid to the customer fails for lack of proof. For businesses with customers from the EU, the same procedure applies in reverse for verifying tax-free refunds.

Record-Keeping and Audit Practice

Business records – and therefore every proof of export – must be retained for ten years (Art. 49 para. 2 VAT Act). In audits the FTA does not check "whether something was exported" by sampling, but whether a document exists for each line item. The clean linkage of commercial invoice, Passar declaration and eVV Export (including XML) is half the VAT audit.

Practical tip: the eVV Export contains the assessment confirmation of the export; anyone who "retrieves and archives it proactively" (the FOCBS recommendation) instead of leaving it in the system avoids the most common trap: goods gone, document never retrieved, tax reclaimed.

Checklist for Exporting Businesses

  1. Passar onboarding done? UID registered in the customs customer administration (ZKV), role "document retrieval in goods traffic" activated in the ePortal?
  2. Retrieval of the eVV Export organised (Chartera Output or customs software) – including archiving the PDF with XML?
  3. Travel traffic: processes defined for export declarations at the border crossing?
  4. Tobacco: transitional rules and Form. 11.44 observed?
  5. Invoicing: 0 % rate with a note referring to the export supply; reference to the customs declaration number?
  6. Retention: documents secured audit-compliantly for 10 years?

In Short

  • VAT exemption on exports = burden of proof on the supplier; without proof, 8.1 % is reassessed.
  • Since 1 January 2026: exports only via Passar; e-dec Export is switched off. The central proof is the eVV Export with digital signature (PDF valid only with embedded XML).
  • Other suitable documents: Form. 11.44 (tobacco), export document in travel traffic, customs procedure documents.
  • Retention duty: 10 years.
  • General information, not individual tax advice – in doubt, consult the FTA or a VAT expert.
