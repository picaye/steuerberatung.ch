VAT rise: what AHV and army cost you

What is on the ballot on 29 November 2026

On 29 November 2026 Swiss voters decide on the «Federal decree of 19 June 2026 on additional AHV financing through a VAT increase» – the funding half of the 13th AHV pension, which the people accepted in March 2024 with 58 %. The first extra pension is paid in December 2026 to some 2.6 million retirees: unmarried pensioners receive up to CHF 2,520 more per year, married couples up to CHF 3,780. The cost to the AHV: over CHF 4 billion initially, more than CHF 5 billion a year from 2035.

Parliament proposes partial financing: the standard rate rises from 8.1 to 8.5 % (+0.4 percentage points) and the special hotel rate from 3.8 to 4.0 %. The reduced rate of 2.6 % on essentials – food, medicine, newspapers, books – stays unchanged; a proposed 0.2-point rise in AHV payroll contributions was narrowly rejected by the National Council (98 to 96). The VAT increase is unlimited in time.

Because the constitution must be amended, the vote is mandatory and requires a double majority of people and cantons; Federal Council and Parliament recommend acceptance. The extra revenue is some CHF 1 billion in 2028 and an estimated CHF 1.4 billion per year from 2029. For perspective: the increase can enter into force no earlier than early 2028 – the 13th pensions of 2026 and 2027 are paid from the AHV compensation fund in any case, without additional funding.

Practical tip: The December 2026 payout does not depend on the vote. A «no» does not scrap the 13th pension – it only postpones the answer of how it gets paid for.

The army fund: a second VAT rise from 2028

The second VAT project is already in parliamentary committee: on 12 August 2026 the Federal Council submitted its message on financing army procurement (FFe 2026 2354, bill 26.051). The proposal: from 1 January 2028 the standard rate rises by a further 0.5 percentage points – limited to 12 years – with the entire yield ring-fenced for armament spending. The accommodation rate would rise proportionally by 0.3 points (0.2 if the AHV decree is accepted). Here too the reduced rate is untouched.

The need is around CHF 24 billion: CHF 15 billion for rapidly building capabilities against the most likely threats (air defence, cyber defence, anti-drone systems, protection of critical infrastructure, ammunition stockpiles), plus CHF 9 billion to absorb price rises on the global armament market. The money flows into a new, indebtedness-capable armament fund (maximum CHF 6 billion of debt, which must be repaid by the end of the 12-year period); Parliament decides how the money is spent.

For planning purposes, both projects add up: the army decree also requires a constitutional amendment and a popular vote – the Federal Council targets summer 2027 so that the increase, like the AHV one, can take effect at the start of 2028. A double «yes» would put the standard rate at 9.0 % from 2028 and the hotel rate at around 4.2 %. The difference: the army points expire after 12 years, the AHV points do not.

Practical tip: Judge the two proposals separately – on 29 November 2026 only the AHV financing is at the ballot box; the army proposal will most likely follow in 2027.

Worked example: what the rise costs a household

What matters is not the percentage point but the price effect: VAT is levied on the gross price. Moving from 8.1 to 8.5 % raises a standard-rated product by 108.5/108.1 = +0.37 %; with an accepted army fund (9.0 % total) it is 109.0/108.1 = +0.83 %. Example of a family with two children: they spend CHF 1,400 a month on standard-rated consumption (restaurants, clothing, fuel, telecoms, hairdresser), CHF 600 a month on reduced-rated essentials and CHF 2,000 a year on hotel nights.

The arithmetic: a «yes» to the AHV decree alone costs this family around CHF 66 per year (CHF 62 on standard-rated spending plus CHF 4 on hotels). If the army fund passes too, the extra cost grows to about CHF 148 per year – roughly CHF 12 a month. The weekly food shop is untouched. The Federal Tax Administration (FTA) estimates the average household burden of the +0.4 points at around CHF 180 per year, based on standard-rated consumption of some CHF 48,500. On the benefits side: a single retiree receives up to CHF 2,520 from the 13th pension while contributing about CHF 180 via VAT – a net plus of roughly CHF 2,340 per year.

A federal tax: the same rate in all 26 cantons

VAT is a purely federal levy: collected by the FTA and remitted to the federal treasury – under the AHV decree, ring-fenced for the AHV compensation fund. That is why the increase applies identically in all 26 cantons: 8.1 % today, 8.5 % from 2028 if accepted – in Zurich as in Chiasso. Unlike income, wealth or property-gain tax, there is no cantonal table for VAT and no tax competition; moving house changes nothing about your consumption burden.

The social buffer is equally uniform: the reduced rate on essentials – food, medicine, newspapers, books, menstrual-hygiene products – stays at 2.6 % in both proposals. Low-income households, which spend a larger share on essentials, are therefore hit relatively less than average. And for cross-border commuters: consumption in Germany, France or Italy is taxed under those countries' VAT – the Swiss increase only affects spending in Switzerland.

Practical tip: Never trust canton-specific forum numbers on VAT – the rate is identical countrywide. Cantonal differences start where direct taxes are concerned: there a comparison is worth real money.

Self-employed: what to prepare before 2028

For VAT-registered self-employed persons the rate change is a bookkeeping matter, not a cost: you invoice the tax to customers and pass it on. Example: on CHF 160,000 of taxable revenue (excl. tax) a hairdressing business remits CHF 12,960 at 8.1 % and CHF 13,600 at 8.5 % – the CHF 640 difference belongs to the treasury, and input tax on your own purchases rises at the same time. Anyone below the CHF 100,000 turnover threshold who is not registered charges no VAT at all.

What remains to be done before the switch: update your till and invoicing software in good time, give long-term contracts and standing invoices a rate-change clause, and from the effective date show gross prices at the new rate under the price-display rules. Small businesses on the flat-rate (balance) method will see the FTA adjust the branch rates; the Federal Council has announced transitional provisions in the VAT act for advance payments and supplies straddling the changeover date. We cover the full rate-change playbook – deposits, partial supplies, period allocation – separately once the date and rates are final.

Practical tip: If you do not have AGOV access to the FTA ePortal yet, set it up now – it has been mandatory for all VAT returns since 31 October 2026 (see the AGOV article). No access, no timely filing, whatever the rate.

What a «no» changes – and what you can do today

If the decree is rejected on 29 November, the 13th pension itself stays – but the financing hole remains. Without extra revenue the annual AHV deficit grows to almost CHF 5 billion by 2035; the AHV fund would sink to around 50 % of one year's expenditure (with the increase: just under 70 % and a deficit of about 3 billion). The Federal Council has already put scenarios out for consultation within the ongoing AHV 2030 reform – including more VAT and lower pensions. The race is tight: the first SRG trend poll (fieldwork to mid-October 2026) had it at 49 % no versus 46 % yes.

For your own numbers: nothing changes until end-2027 (8.1 / 2.6 / 3.8 %). Anyone budgeting for 2028 prices the effect in as a consumer – and can offset it where the tax leverage is largest: a pillar 3a contribution (up to CHF 7,258 in 2026 with a pension-fund affiliation) typically saves several times what the VAT increase costs a household per year. The 3a calculator shows your personal maximum; the tax checklist bundles the year-end deadlines.

In short:

  • 29.11.2026: popular vote on +0.4 VAT points for the 13th AHV pension (standard 8.1 → 8.5 %, hotel 3.8 → 4.0 %, essentials unchanged at 2.6 %).
  • In force no earlier than 1.1.2028 – the 13th pensions of 2026/2027 are paid either way.
  • Army fund: +0.5 points limited to 12 years, vote expected summer 2027 → with a double yes the standard rate becomes 9.0 %.
  • Household impact: the FTA estimates ~CHF 180 per year (AHV proposal); our example family pays CHF 66–148.
  • No cantonal differences – VAT is uniform across Switzerland.
