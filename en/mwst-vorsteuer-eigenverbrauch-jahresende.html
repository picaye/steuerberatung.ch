Why the year-end VAT reconciliation is due now

Every VAT-liable person filing quarterly must submit the settlement block for the third quarter of 2026 (July–September) within 60 days and pay the tax within the same period – the deadline is 30 November 2026 (Art. 71 para. 1 and Art. 86 para. 1 VAT Act). Corrections discovered late – e.g. undeclared self-consumption – are simply reported in this settlement. The ESTV does not send reminders; the 60-day rule applies automatically to every period (details: VAT settlements: deadlines per period).

Year-end reconciliation means comparing your VAT settlements with the annual financial statements: deficiencies identified must be corrected no later than in the settlement covering the 180th day after the end of the business year (Art. 72 VAT Act) – i.e. by the end of June the following year for calendar business years.

The ESTV closes the book as well: if no corrective settlement arrives within 240 days after the end of the business year, it assumes the settlements were complete and correct and finalises the tax period. Later corrections in the company's favour then become considerably more laborious.

Practical tip: Set the closing date with your bookkeeping team at the end of November. Corrections for self-consumption, gifts and change of use then land in the 30.11. settlement – rather than in a corrective settlement the following year, which can also trigger default interest.

Business vehicles: the 0.9% per month flat rate for private use

If an employee may use a business vehicle privately, this constitutes a withdrawal from the business in VAT terms: the private use is self-consumption under Art. 31 para. 2 VAT Act, taxable at the standard rate (8.1% since 2024). Accepted is the flat rate aligned with the salary certificate: 0.9% of the purchase price per month – including special equipment, excluding VAT – as applied by the ESTV since 1 January 2022 (previously 0.8%). For vehicles with a purchase price below CHF 16,667, a minimum of CHF 150 per month applies.

Worked example: a new car costs CHF 45’000 net of VAT. The private-use component is 0.9% × CHF 45’000 = CHF 405 per month, i.e. CHF 4’860 per year. Per quarter (tax base CHF 1,215) the company owes 8.1% = CHF 98.42, or CHF 393.66 per year – reported in each ordinary settlement. Anyone with less private use may declare a lower amount but must document it properly (mileage log).

Practical tip: The flat rate has a double effect – the same private-use component appears as salary item 2.2 on the salary certificate (subject to AHV/IV/EO/ALV contributions) and as self-consumption in the VAT settlement. Check both positions at year-end: in practice the salary certificate gets updated and the VAT side is forgotten.

Customer gifts: the CHF 500 allowance per person per year

Gift baskets, wine, giveaways: gratuitous transfers from the business are in principle self-consumption – exception under Art. 31 para. 2 lit. c VAT Act: for gifts up to CHF 500 per person per calendar year (value net of VAT) the business reason is presumed without further ado and no VAT is due. The same applies to advertising gifts and samples used to generate taxable turnover.

Anyone exceeding the threshold taxes the full amount as self-consumption – not just the excess. Example: a customer gift with a purchase value of CHF 600 costs about CHF 48.60 in VAT at 8.1%. The profit-tax rules use the same ceiling: customer gifts are deductible only up to CHF 500 per recipient per year.

Practical tip: At Christmas, keep a gift list per recipient and add up all transfers during the calendar year. The allowance is “per person and year” – two gifts of CHF 300 to the same person are already fully taxable.

Change of use of business property: the 20-year correction

For real estate too, actual use decides on the input tax deduction: if the requirements lapse afterwards – e.g. because an office previously used for taxable purposes is rented out for private residential use without option, i.e. tax-exempt – the input tax deducted must be repaid at its value in time (Art. 31 paras. 1 and 3 VAT Act). The deduction is reduced linearly by one twentieth per elapsed year for real estate (a correction over 20 years, 5% each) and one fifth for movable assets and services (5 years). Accounting treatment is irrelevant.

Worked example: a company builds an office in 2019; construction costs CHF 2 million net of VAT, input tax of CHF 154’000 deducted (2019 rate: 7.7%). From 1 January 2026, 40% of the floor space is rented tax-exemptly to private tenants. Calculation: 40% of CHF 154,000 = CHF 61,600; 7 elapsed years (2019–2025) cut 35%, leaving 65% → CHF 40,040 repayable – corrected in the settlement of the period in which the change of use occurs.

The reverse mechanism is the tax removal on private assets brought in (Einlageentsteuerung, Art. 32 VAT Act): if entitlement to the deduction arises only later, input tax can be reclaimed using the same value-in-time mechanism. And a landlord who wants to keep tax-exempt rentals inside the VAT system can opt to declare them taxable (Art. 22 VAT Act) – the deduction then remains intact. Practical tip: keep documentation on the use of business property for at least 20 years – the usual ten-year retention period is not enough for these corrections.

Year-end checklist: VAT corrections on time and interest-free

  • By 30 November 2026: file and pay the Q3 settlement – late self-consumption corrections (vehicles, gifts, mixed costs) belong in this settlement.
  • Year-end reconciliation: compare VAT settlements with the financial statements; correct deficiencies no later than in the settlement containing the 180th day after the end of the business year (Art. 72 VAT Act).
  • Keep the ESTV's 240-day practice in view: after that the period counts as finalised – corrections in your favour then require considerably more effort.
  • Delay costs money: default interest without reminder (Art. 87 VAT Act), an assessed tax claim where records are missing (Art. 79 VAT Act) and fines under the criminal provisions (Art. 96 et seq. VAT Act). The right to assess tax claims only becomes time-barred after five years (Art. 42 VAT Act) – ignoring the issue does not help.
  • Check access: since 31 October 2026, VAT settlements run mandatorily via AGOV (AGOV requirement: how to log in).
  • Plan for the rate change: to finance the AHV, the standard rate is set to rise to 8.5% – this article shows what it means for your settlements.

Whether company car, Christmas present or the conversion of shop space – year-end corrections are small-scale work with big leverage, because mistakes turn into cash VAT payments immediately. Check the three classics in this article (vehicle flat rate, gift list, change of use) before 30 November; for real-estate cases, consult a verified professional beforehand.
