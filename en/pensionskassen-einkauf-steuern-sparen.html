Pension Fund Buy-In: The Tax Deduction and the 3-Year Trap

Why the Pension Fund Buy-In Is the Strongest Tax Lever

Voluntary buy-ins to a pension fund (2nd pillar) are fully deductible from taxable income – at federal, cantonal and municipal level. Unlike pillar 3a, they are not capped at CHF 7'258: what matters is your buy-in potential, which depending on age, salary and the fund's regulations can quickly reach CHF 50'000, CHF 100'000 or more.

The mechanism: you close a pension gap, increase your future annuity or retirement capital – and save tax immediately in the year you pay in. On payout, the capital is taxed separately at a significantly lower rate (capital withdrawal tax). That differential is exactly what makes the buy-in attractive.

Practical tip: your buy-in potential is shown on your pension fund statement (often labelled «possible buy-in» or «buy-in potential»). If you don't have it, request it from your pension fund – without the figure, nothing works.

The 3-Year Blocking Period (Art. 79b para. 3 BVG/Occa)

This is where most people get caught out: within three years of a buy-in you may not withdraw any capital from the 2nd pillar. If you miss the deadline, the tax authorities open a reassessment (follow-up tax) procedure – the deduction is reversed and you pay back the income tax at all levels, plus default interest.

The blocking period applies to all forms of capital withdrawal from the 2nd pillar:

  • Capital withdrawal at retirement
  • WEF withdrawal for home ownership (home-ownership promotion)
  • Withdrawal on emigration (exception: mandatory insurance within EU/EFTA)
  • Withdrawal on starting self-employment

The period starts on the exact day of the buy-in. So plan at least three years between your last buy-in and any planned capital withdrawal – and correspondingly earlier if retirement is near.

Exception: if at retirement you take an annuity instead of capital, the blocking period does not apply. Buy-ins after a divorce to restore the pension are also exempt under pension law (Art. 79b para. 4 BVG/Occa) – but the tax office will still check whether tax avoidance is involved.

WEF Withdrawal: Repay First, Then Buy In

If you previously withdrew pension fund money for home ownership (WEF), a double restriction applies: the WEF withdrawal must be fully repaid before you can make tax-effective buy-ins. And the 3-year blocking period applies on top – a WEF withdrawal within three years of a buy-in voids the deduction.

The pleasant side effect: when you repay the WEF withdrawal, you can reclaim the lump-sum withdrawal tax you paid at the time (application within the deadline to the authority that paid it out). This is often forgotten and can quickly return four- or five-figure amounts.

Practical tip: plan the sequence – first repay the WEF withdrawal (reclaim the lump-sum withdrawal tax), then check your buy-in potential, then buy in, then withdraw nothing for three years.

Worked Example: How Much You Really Save

Example: Marc, 55, has a buy-in potential of CHF 120'000 and a marginal tax rate of around 30%. He pays in three tranches of CHF 40'000 over three years (tax progression smoothing – each year lowers the rate again).

Tax saving per tranche: around CHF 12'000, totalling approx. CHF 36'000. At retirement at 65 he takes the capital; the capital withdrawal tax, depending on canton and municipality, is typically well below the income tax rate – often in the 5–10% range. The bottom line is a five-figure saving – plus a higher annuity or retirement capital.

Two risks belong in the honest calculation: (1) Underfunding – if you buy into a pension fund with a low funding ratio, it may later need restructuring (pension cuts, mandatory contributions). (2) Conversion rate – if it falls, your buy-in yields less pension than expected. Check both figures in your pension fund statement or annual report.

Checklist: 5 Steps to the Buy-In

  1. Get your pension fund statement and check the buy-in potential, funding ratio and conversion rate.
  2. Check for an outstanding WEF withdrawal: if open, repay it first (and reclaim the lump-sum withdrawal tax).
  3. Work backwards on timing: when do you want to withdraw capital? Your last buy-in must be at least 3 years before that.
  4. Spread the buy-in over several years instead of one large sum – that smooths tax progression.
  5. Pay in before the end of the year and keep the pension fund's confirmation for your tax return.

The buy-in is one of the few measures that improves retirement provision and taxes at the same time – but only if the deadlines and sequence are right.
