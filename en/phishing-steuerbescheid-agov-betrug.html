Fake Tax Emails and AGOV Phishing: How to Spot Scams Impersonating the Tax Administration

Published on 2026-10-09 · Independent tax information for Switzerland.

Contents:
  1. The FTA is warning: scam emails in the name of the tax administration
  2. The classic trap: the phantom tax refund
  3. Fraudulent calls and vishing: spotting the callback trap
  4. The AGOV switch: why phishing waves follow deadline changes
  5. What to do if it happens — and what not to do

The FTA is warning: scam emails in the name of the tax administration

The Federal Tax Administration (FTA/ESTV) continuously publishes warnings on its “Digital threats” page about fraudulent messages posing as FTA emails. Most recently identified sender domains include @federaltaxadministration.com and @ftaswiss.live, following earlier alerts about addresses such as @miumg.edu.gt and kundenportal@hilfe-kundendienst.ch. The FTA states these lists are not exhaustive — new spoofed addresses keep appearing.

The simplest protection is a rule the federal authorities themselves publish: official emails from Swiss public authorities are always sent from addresses ending in @admin.ch (or a subdomain of it). Anything else — .com, .live, or .ch addresses belonging to unrelated companies — is not a government message. The FTA's instruction is unambiguous: do not click links, do not open attachments, do not share personal, financial or tax information.

Practical tip: In every “tax” email, check the sender in full, not just the display name. Scammers type “FTA” or “Cantonal tax office” into the sender line while the technical address points to a foreign domain. When in doubt, delete the message and open the official portal by typing the address yourself — never via a link in the email.

The classic trap: the phantom tax refund

The most common scheme is bait with a refund: the FTA describes a fraudulent message announcing an alleged tax refund of CHF 287, designed to lure victims into entering bank details on a fake government website. The trick works through relief and carelessness — CHF 287 sounds small and plausible, and an IBAN form sounds like the standard refund process.

Key fact: a genuine withholding-tax refund or a cantonal assessment refund is never “transferred” to a private post account and never processed via a form in an email. Refunds run through your tax return or assessment and are paid to the account already known to the authority. If you genuinely expect a refund, check the status directly in the official ePortal or with your cantonal tax office — using the manually typed official domain, not a link.

Practical tip: Remember “a refund needs no link, no form, no new IBAN”. Any message that demands bank or login details under time pressure (“confirm within 72 hours”) for money credited to you is fraud. Official decisions do not come with countdown timers.

Fraudulent calls and vishing: spotting the callback trap

Beyond email, the FTA warns about fraudulent phone calls made in its name requesting a callback, and about vishing — computer-generated calls designed to appear genuine in order to harvest data. The authority is explicit: ignore such calls and do not call back.

Its concrete protection advice: never disclose sensitive data by phone — even if the caller already knows correct personal details (that is only a trust trick); end the call immediately if there is pressure, threats or an offer of remote access; ignore automated computer voices, especially at unusual hours; and if in doubt, look up the official phone number yourself and call that — never a number given by the caller.

Practical tip: No tax office or the FTA ever asks by phone for your AVS number, IBAN or credentials, and no authority offers to remote-control your computer. Remote access in a “tax” call is fraud without exception — hang up and report it to the police.

The AGOV switch: why phishing waves follow deadline changes

The FTA is restructuring its ePortal services: since May 2026, VAT returns can only be filed through “VAT return pro”, and from 31 October 2026 registration and login in the ePortal are mandatory via the AGOV government login; the previous CH-Login will be fully deactivated at the end of 2027 (FTA media release of 6 October 2025). Every VAT-liable company must therefore act before the end of October — exactly the urgency scammers exploit with fake portals and “your login expires” emails.

The legitimate route runs only through the official domains: www.agov.admin.ch for the government login and FTA services via www.estv.admin.ch or the ePortal linked there. AGOV itself works without classic passwords, using device-based login and app confirmation — so an “reset your AGOV password” link in an email already contradicts the system. Technical help comes from the official FTA service desk at +41 58 461 61 11; guides are available at help.eiam.swiss.

Practical tip: Register AGOV early — not on the deadline day. Once the switch is done, “confirm your login by …” emails read as noise rather than urgency. Save the official URLs as bookmarks and report phishing attempts to the National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC), which the FTA warning page links as the further point of contact.

What to do if it happens — and what not to do

If you clicked a link but entered no data: take no other action, forward the message to the NCSC reporting channel and delete it — precisely the procedure the FTA prescribes (“report the message according to the applicable procedures and delete it”). If you did disclose bank or login data, speed matters: have your bank block accounts immediately, change every account sharing the password, and report the incident to the police and the NCSC.

Companies are equally affected: a compromised ePortal login is not just an IT problem — it touches VAT filing and signing authority for the business. Notify the FTA via its official contact channel and have the permissions under myESTV reset. And remember: neither the FTA nor cantonal tax offices send reminders or assessments as email attachments with payment demands — official documents arrive by post or through the official delivery portal.

Practical tip: Keep the official warning page as a reference: estv.admin.ch/de/digitale-bedrohungen lists the identified spoofed addresses and schemes, updated continuously. Forward it to employees, parents and club treasurers — people over 55 are hit disproportionately by the “refund” emails.
