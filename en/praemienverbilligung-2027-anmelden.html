2027 premiums up 5.0 % — a good reason to check your subsidy entitlement

The Federal Office of Public Health announced on 29 September 2026 that the average health-insurance premium will rise to CHF 412 per month in 2027 — an increase of 5.0 % or CHF 19.70 compared with 2026. The average monthly premium for adults is CHF 487.60 (+4.9 %), for young adults CHF 338.80 and for children CHF 128.10. Where premiums rise, low-income households are hit hardest — which is precisely where the individual premium subsidy (IPV) steps in.

Under the IPV, cantons reduce premiums for insured persons in modest economic circumstances. The money never runs through your own bank account: the canton pays the subsidy directly to your health insurer, which deducts it from your monthly premium bill. In 2023 some 2.5 million insured people — roughly 28 % of the basic-insurance population — received a subsidy, including recipients of supplementary benefits and social assistance.

Practical tip: if you last checked your entitlement two or three years ago, check again this week — several cantons close the 2027 application window within the coming weeks.

Who qualifies — the cantons decide, the Confederation only sets minimum standards

Article 65 of the Health Insurance Act obliges cantons to grant premium reductions to insured persons in modest economic circumstances. Almost everything else is a cantonal matter: who counts as “modest”, how large the subsidy is, which income and asset thresholds apply, and which deadlines and forms are used — all decided by the 26 cantons. Two households with identical income can therefore be treated very differently depending on where they live.

Federal minimum requirements exist only for families: for lower and middle incomes, cantons must subsidise children's premiums by at least 80 % and premiums of young adults in training by at least 50 %. Funding comes from both levels of government: the Confederation contributes 7.5 % of gross basic-insurance costs, and since 1 January 2026 the cantons are obliged to fund a minimum share of their own cantonal costs. That duty is the indirect counter-proposal to the premium relief initiative, which voters and cantons rejected on 9 June 2024 with 55.5 % No; during a two-year transition period the minimum cantonal share equals 3.5 % of cantonal gross costs.

Practical tip: check the income thresholds of your canton of residence — most cantonal compensation offices offer a free, non-binding online calculator. It takes less than two minutes.

2027 deadlines: several cantons close applications at the end of October

First, the point that matters most: in several cantons the application window for 2027 closes within weeks. Lucerne requires applications by 31 October 2026; anyone applying later receives the subsidy only from the following month, and retroactive applications are excluded. In Aargau the online application for 2027 is open until 31 December 2026 — people likely to qualify automatically receive a registration code from September 2026, and everyone else can order one. Schwyz shows how tight it can get: there the 31 December of the year concerned is a true forfeiture deadline — for 2026 that means 31 December 2026 — and depending on when the application arrives the subsidy can take effect retroactively as of 1 January.

Other cantons are more generous: in Zurich, people expected to qualify automatically receive an application form; anyone who did not get one can still file until end of March 2027. In St. Gallen the deadline for a full-year claim is 31 March 2027; later applications are calculated only from the month of filing. These dates cannot be generalised — for many cantons 31 December 2026 is the hard cut. Dates verified on the cantonal compensation offices' websites as of 2 October 2026; the official page of your canton of residence always governs.

Practical tip: put 31 October 2026 in your calendar as the earliest possible deadline and find out this week which date applies in your canton. A missed subsidy costs money for an entire year — and in most cantons it cannot be made up.

How to apply — and how the money is paid out

Whether you must apply, and how, is again decided by the canton. Some determine entitlement automatically from tax data and pay without an application; others send a pre-filled form or a code; others again require an active application (in Zurich, for instance, until the end of March of the following year at the compensation office SVA). The money never passes through your private account: the canton transfers the subsidy directly to your insurer, and your monthly bill shows the corresponding deduction.

In cantons with a personal-participation model such as Zurich, what you pay yourself consists of a flat basic contribution plus an income-dependent share. For 2027 Zurich sets the reference premium at 70 % of the regional average premium and the basic contribution at 30 %; the participation rate is 11.8 % of relevant income for married couples and registered partners, and 9.4 % for single and lone-parent households. Families have separate ceilings: children's premiums are subsidised by at least 80 % where relevant income is up to CHF 71'200 (up to CHF 94'900 if at least one young adult is in training) and total taxable assets stay below CHF 300'000.

If your circumstances change during the year — marriage, separation, birth, job loss — you can request a recomputation: under Article 65 para. 3 of the Act, cantons must take the most recent income and family circumstances into account on application.

Practical tip: file the change request immediately after the event, not in the next cycle. Every month of waiting is money lost.

Premium subsidy and tax return: what belongs together — and what does not

The subsidy itself is not income: you do not declare the cantonal contribution as revenue in the tax return. What matters fiscally is only the part you bear yourself — the flat-rate deduction for insurance premiums and savings interest covers the premium actually paid, so a heavily subsidised premium also shrinks the deduction. At federal level the flat rate is capped at CHF 1'800 for single taxpayers (CHF 2'700 for those without pillar 2 or 3a contributions), plus CHF 700 per child; jointly assessed couples with pension contributions fall under a higher federal cap, and many cantons allow considerably more than the Confederation — see the guide to your own tax return.

Do not mix the two levels: the claim to the subsidy exists independently of the tax return, and conversely the insurance deduction on the return does not create an entitlement to a subsidy. Checking both — the cantonal deadline before Christmas and the deductions in spring — covers the topic completely. The other key deductions are in our guides Tax-saving tips and the 2026 tax checklist.

Practical tip: keep premium bills and subsidy decisions in one folder — the compensation office, your insurer and the tax authority may all ask for the same documents.

In short

  • Average 2027 premium: CHF 412 per month, +5.0 % (FOPH press release of 29.9.2026).
  • Entitlement, amount and deadlines are cantonal; federal minimums: children at least 80 %, young adults in training at least 50 % subsidised.
  • First windows close: Lucerne 31.10.2026, Aargau 31.12.2026, Zurich and St. Gallen until end of March 2027 — as of 2.10.2026, check your own canton.
  • The subsidy is paid directly to your insurer; late applications usually count only from the month filed.
  • On change of circumstances: request a recomputation (Art. 65 para. 3 HIA) — don't wait.
  • The subsidy is tax-neutral; only the premium you actually bear counts in the tax return.
