Source tax: claim additional deductions by 31 March

Why the source tax rate deducts too little

The source tax deduction is a flat-rate final tax: the rate only includes professional expenses, insurance premiums and deductions on a lump-sum basis. Anyone with higher actual costs – effective professional expenses instead of the flat rate, illness and accident costs, further training, third-party childcare costs, pillar 3a contributions, maintenance payments – sees none of it in the monthly payslip.

These additional deductions can only be obtained through the subsequent ordinary assessment (NOV): the tax authority assesses you as in the ordinary procedure, credits the source tax already paid, and refunds the difference if it turned out too high.

The deadline: 31 March of the following year

The application for a subsequent ordinary assessment is tied to a hard deadline: it must reach the cantonal tax authority by the end of March of the year following the tax period (FTA information sheet «Besteuerung an der Quelle»; likewise the canton of Zurich's source tax ordinance: «bis Ende März des Folgejahres» – until the end of March of the following year; confirmed by the Fribourg and Thurgau tax authorities for period practice). For the 2026 tax period the deadline expires on 31 March 2027.

Anyone who misses the deadline loses the deductions without a trace: without an NOV, the source tax definitively replaces the federal, cantonal and communal taxes on employment income – no additional deductions are granted afterwards.

Who must apply, who should apply

The NOV is mandatory for persons subject to source tax with domicile or residence in Switzerland if their gross income exceeds CHF 120'000 per year (FTA information sheet). Here the assessment is made ex officio – the application itself is unnecessary, but the additional deductions must still be claimed.

Voluntarily, the application pays off for everyone else whose actual deductions exceed the flat allowances built into the rate. Typical candidates: people with high real travel and meal costs, families with third-party childcare, people with illness costs above the deductible franchises, payers of maintenance.

Non-residents (cross-border commuters, persons staying abroad) can also apply for the NOV to claim personal deductions – for them the procedure is the only route to these deductions; the application must likewise be filed by 31 March of the following year.

Correction vs. additional deductions: two different applications

Not every correction needs an NOV: if the gross salary was determined wrongly, the income governing the rate was calculated incorrectly, or the wrong rate was applied, an application to recalculate the source tax suffices (Art. 137 FATA, Art. 49 para. 2–2ter of the Tax Harmonisation Act) – also by 31 March of the following year. The NOV, by contrast, opens up the entire deduction logic of ordinary assessment, including personal circumstances (marital status, children, assets).

How the application works

The form is available from the cantonal tax authority (in many cantons it is available online every year from 1 January to 31 March). Married couples and registered partnerships must sign the application jointly; it must state the tax period. Enclose the usual supporting documents: salary certificates, proof of professional expenses, illness costs, 3a contributions, childcare and maintenance costs. The assessment follows ordinary law; the source tax withheld is credited. The FTA normally issues the assessment by the end of the application year – after that, the ordinary remedies run (objection within 30 days).

Calculation example: when the application pays off

Rule of thumb: the source tax rate contains a flat allowance for professional expenses. If the actual costs (commuting, meals, further training, home office) are clearly above it, the deduction rises by the difference. By way of example: someone who can prove CHF 12'000 of real professional expenses instead of the rate's flat allowance of a few thousand francs lowers their taxable income accordingly – at a marginal rate of 15–25 % that means a refund in the range of CHF 1'500–3'000 per year. The exact calculation depends on canton, commune, marital status and children; cantonal tax authorities and cantonal tax software allow a rough estimate before you apply.

In short

  • Additional deductions (actual professional expenses, illness, further training, third-party childcare, pillar 3a, maintenance) are only available with a subsequent ordinary assessment (NOV).
  • Deadline: 31 March of the following year – for the 2026 period until 31.3.2027. Without an application, the deductions lapse.
  • Mandatory from gross income above CHF 120'000 (residents); voluntary for everyone else, including cross-border commuters.
  • Wrong rate/incorrect salary → recalculation application (Art. 137 FATA / Art. 49 Tax Harmonisation Act), same deadline.
  • General information, not individual advice – the cantonal tax authority provides guidance.
