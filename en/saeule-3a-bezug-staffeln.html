Pillar 3a withdrawal: Spread payments over several years to save tax

Why staged withdrawals save tax

When you withdraw your pillar 3a savings, the capital is taxed separately: lump-sum benefits from recognised tied provision are assessed separately from all other income, at one-fifth of the ordinary rate (Art. 38 FATA; FTA guide 2026). That sounds mild — but it is progressive: a single withdrawal of CHF 200'000 costs proportionally far more than five withdrawals of CHF 40'000 in five different years.

So the saving lever is not the reduced rate itself but the splitting across tax periods. Art. 38 FATA states that each capital benefit is always subject to a "full annual tax" — but only for the year in which it accrues. Spreading withdrawals over several years breaks the progression again and again.

Practical tip: Do not start planning in your retirement year. The legal framework allows a withdrawal window of up to eleven years (see below) — actively use the years before retirement for staged redemptions.

The withdrawal window: earlier than you think

Pillar 3a assets may be withdrawn no earlier than five years before the reference age (Art. 3 para. 1 OPP 3). For men (reference age 65) the window opens at 60; for women, depending on year of birth, already at 59 — the reference age for women is being raised in stages from 64 to 65 under the AHV 21 reform: for the 1962 cohort it is 64 years and 6 months in 2026; from the 1964 cohort onward, 65 applies for women as well (FSO).

And you do not have to withdraw early: if you remain gainfully employed after reaching the reference age, you may postpone the withdrawal by up to five years — giving you up to eleven tax years for staging in the best case. Note: the capital becomes due at reference age. If you keep working beyond it and stop before age 70, every remaining pillar 3a account must be closed and taxed at the end of that employment (FTA Circular 18a).

Practical tip: Work out your personal window: earliest withdrawal = reference age minus five years. Retiring at 63? You can close the first account at that age and distribute the rest over the following years.

One account = one withdrawal: the key rule

A single pillar 3a account can only be closed in full: partial withdrawals are not permitted. If you request a payout within the five years before the reference age, the entire account balance including interest is captured for tax — even if the bank transfers only part of it. Retroactively splitting an existing balance into several accounts is not possible, as the FTA makes clear.

The consequence: you must hold several accounts before you reach your target years. Anyone saving in one large deposit today forfeits the staging option. As a rule of thumb: open a second account once your balance passes about CHF 50'000, a third above CHF 100'000 — and allocate new contributions across all accounts.

Practical tip: Open the additional pillar 3a account ideally before year-end and pay your next contribution into it. Opening an account is free with most providers; just check whether yours charges fees on low balances.

Caution: the same tax period is aggregated

Staging only works if withdrawals fall into different tax years. Capital benefits that become due in the same tax year are added together and taxed with a single annual tax — the cantonal execution authorities (including the Zurich and Lucern tax-office directives) are unanimous on this. In concrete terms:

  • Do not take your pension fund and pillar 3a capital in the same year — benefits from both pillars are aggregated and push the rate up.
  • Close only as many accounts per year as are budget-neutral — two accounts closed in one year are taxed together.
  • The same applies to married couples: while ordinary joint assessment governs, both partners' withdrawals in the same year are taxed together.

Home ownership promotion (WEF) also follows a five-year rhythm: a withdrawal for buying, building or repaying a mortgage is only possible every five years, and only up to five years before the reference age — beyond that age, only the entire benefit from that provision relationship may be withdrawn (Art. 3 OPP 3, Circular 18a).

Worked example: staging at the federal tax level

An example using the official FTA tariff table 2026 (single taxpayer; cantonal and municipal rates differ, but the mechanism is the same): a pension capital of CHF 200'000 withdrawn in a single year costs CHF 12'903.35 in full annual federal tax. Distributing the same capital over four withdrawals of CHF 50'000 in four years costs CHF 400.80 per year — CHF 1'603.20 in total.

The difference of over CHF 11'000 arises because the pension tariff (one-fifth of the ordinary rate) is extremely progressive: the tax rises more than proportionally even at modest amounts. Cantonal rates are lower but follow the same pattern.

Practical tip: Have your cantonal tax office or a licensed partner look up the cantonal lump-sum withdrawal rates for your specific amount. Rule of thumb: the larger the total capital, the more years you should plan for.

In short

  1. Pillar 3a capital benefits are taxed separately — at one-fifth of the rate, but progressively (Art. 38 FATA).
  2. Withdrawal window: from five years before the reference age up to five years after it (if you keep working).
  3. An account is only ever closed in full; splitting existing balances is not possible — the accounts must exist beforehand.
  4. Withdrawals in the same tax year (including pension fund capital) are aggregated — spread them over different years.
  5. In the federal-tax example, CHF 200'000 staged over four years saves over CHF 11'000.
