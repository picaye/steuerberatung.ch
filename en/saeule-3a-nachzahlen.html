Retroactive pillar 3a contributions: closing the gap from 2026

Published on 2026-09-15 · Independent tax information for Switzerland.

What changes in 2026

Until now the rule was simple: if you did not pay the full amount into pillar 3a, the tax deduction was lost for good. Since 1 January 2026 that has changed. The revised OPP 3 ordinance (BVV 3) allows retroactive purchases into tied pension provision – so you can close earlier contribution gaps and reclaim the deduction you missed.

The key points: gaps can be made up up to 10 years retroactively, but only for contribution years from 2025 onwards. Anyone who paid in too little in 2023 or 2024 can no longer correct it. The first retroactive purchase actually possible is therefore in tax year 2026 (for the 2025 gap).

Practical tip: check your 2025 pillar 3a account statements now. Did you pay in less than CHF 7'258 even though you had income subject to AHV contributions? Then you have a contribution gap – and you can close it in 2026.

The requirements – where it often falls through

The retroactive purchase does not happen automatically; strict conditions apply:

  • Regular contribution first: you must have paid the full annual contribution for the current year before you are allowed to close a gap.
  • Income subject to AHV contributions: both in the year of the retroactive purchase and in the gap year.
  • Cap: per gap year you can back-pay at most the "small" maximum amount – currently CHF 7'258. People without a pension fund (20% rule, max. CHF 36'288) can likewise only use the small amount per year for back-payments.
  • Involve your provider: the purchase goes through your 3a bank or insurer; depending on the provider you will need an application or a form.

Worked example: how much you can back-pay

In 2025 Nora paid only CHF 3'500 into pillar 3a – she could have contributed the maximum of CHF 7'258. Her 2025 contribution gap is CHF 3'758.

In 2026 Nora first pays the full regular contribution (CHF 7'258) and then the back-payment of CHF 3'758. A total of CHF 11'016 flows into her pension provision – and both amounts reduce her 2026 taxable income, at federal, cantonal and municipal level.

How much that is worth in francs depends on your marginal tax rate: at 25% progression, a payment of CHF 11'016 saves roughly CHF 2'750 in tax. On top of that, the money keeps working tax-free, and you are taxed on it separately and at a lower rate when it is paid out.

Deadlines and pitfalls

The payment must be value-dated with the provider by 31 December – merely instructing the transfer is not enough. Depending on the bank, allow a few working days of buffer; anyone who transfers on 30 December risks the amount being booked only in the new year.

Further pitfalls: (1) back-payment without the full regular contribution – rejected. (2) gap year before 2025 – not eligible for back-payment. (3) no income subject to AHV contributions in the gap year (e.g. during studies or abroad) – not a gap within the meaning of the ordinance. (4) multiple 3a accounts: what counts is the sum of your contributions per year, not the individual account.

Practical tip: have your 3a provider confirm your purchase potential in writing – that leaves no room for discussion during the tax assessment.

3a or pension fund? Which pays off more

Both are tax-effective purchases, but they differ: the 3a retroactive purchase is cheap, flexible and limited to CHF 7'258 per year. The purchase into the pension fund can be significantly larger (depending on your purchase potential), but is less flexible – and carries a 3-year lock-up period for capital withdrawals.

Rule of thumb: exhaust pillar 3a first (including back-payments), then – if you are close to retirement and want to take capital – factor in the lock-up period before making larger pension fund purchases.
