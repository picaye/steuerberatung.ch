Tax checklist 2026: file your Swiss tax return in 10 steps

Why use a checklist?

The tax return looks like a mountain every year – yet with the right order it takes one to two hours. What matters is having all documents ready before you start and not forgetting a single deduction. This checklist walks you through the 2026 tax return (filed in 2027) step by step.

Practical tip: create a digital folder «Taxes 2026» and collect receipts continuously. By March 2027 everything will be at hand – and nothing gets lost.

Steps 1–3: gather your documents

1. Salary certificates: collect all 2026 salary statements (including side jobs). Check field 11 (net salary) and any expenses/fringe benefits.

2. Bank and securities statements: account balances and interest statements as of 31.12.2026 (most banks issue the «tax statement» automatically), portfolio extracts, securities details. Do not forget small accounts, children's youth accounts or crypto holdings – cryptocurrencies must be declared as wealth (FTA rate list).

3. Deduction receipts: pillar 3a confirmations, health-insurance premiums (annual statement), donation certificates, training costs, public-transport passes, childcare costs (day-care/childminder invoices), property maintenance costs, debt-interest statements.

Steps 4–6: fill in and claim deductions

4. Personal data & income: check master data (civil status, religion, children), enter income from salary, pension, side income and property. For children: their assets (e.g. savings accounts, apprenticeship wage) belong in the joint return.

5. Claim every deduction: pillar 3a, occupational expenses (commuting, meals, other costs, training), child deduction and third-party childcare, health-insurance premiums, donations, debt interest, maintenance costs, dual-earner deduction. Use flat rates where they are higher than your receipts – and vice versa.

6. Do not forget the withholding tax: if you have investment income, declare it together with the 35% retention in your tax return – the refund runs through your canton of residence and is granted for a correct declaration only (deadline: 3 years).

Steps 7–8: check and file

7. Check: spot-check your figures (total salary = sum of all salary certificates? all accounts declared?). In many cantons the filing software flags gaps – take warnings seriously.

8. Sign and submit: online (e-filing) is fastest and gives you instant confirmation. On paper: sign by hand – married couples both! Incomplete signatures are a common cause of delays.

Deadline: generally 31 March of the following year or 30 days after receiving the questionnaire. Too tight? A deadline extension is available free online in almost all cantons (until summer or autumn depending on canton).

Steps 9–10: review the assessment and optimise

9. Review the assessment: when the final tax bill arrives, compare it with your return. Were all deductions accepted? If something is wrong: file an objection within 30 days – free of charge and often successful.

10. Optimise for next year: note what was missing: pay pillar 3a early (before year-end!), check a pension-fund buy-in, plan renovations and spread them over two years, bundle donations. Tackle these points now and you will automatically save more in 2027.
