Saving tax in Switzerland: what you need to know

The 5 biggest levers – at a glance

Saving tax is not rocket science: Swiss tax law allows a whole range of deductions that many taxpayers simply never claim. For employees, the five biggest levers are pillar 3a, professional expenses, child-related deductions, health-insurance premiums and donations. Used consistently, they easily save several thousand francs a year depending on canton and income.

Important to understand: deductions reduce your taxable income, not the tax bill directly. How much you actually save depends on your marginal tax rate – roughly speaking, the higher your income, the more every deducted franc is worth.

Practical tip: keep receipts throughout the year in one folder (digital is fine). Anyone hunting for documents in March forgets deductions – and gives away money.

Pillar 3a: the easiest tax-saving move

Contributions to tied pension provision (pillar 3a) are fully deductible from taxable income. For 2026: up to CHF 7,258 per year for employees with a pension fund. Without a pension fund (e.g. many self-employed), up to 20% of earned income, maximum CHF 36,288.

The trick: the money grows tax-free, and you only pay tax on withdrawal – separately and at a much lower rate. For most people, pillar 3a is therefore the single most lucrative deduction.

Mind the deadline: the contribution must reach your 3a account before the end of the year (allow a few banking days of buffer depending on the provider). Paid in too late = deduction missed.

Professional expenses: commuting, home office, training

Occupational costs are deductible at federal level and in every canton. The main items for 2026:

  • Commuting costs: public transport costs (GA/half-fare/route pass) fully deductible; capped at CHF 3,300 for federal direct tax, sometimes higher at cantonal level.
  • Other occupational expenses: flat rate of 3% of net salary, minimum CHF 2,000, maximum CHF 4,000 (federal tax). Higher actual costs can be claimed with receipts.
  • Further training: job-related courses deductible up to CHF 13,000 per year (federal tax).
  • Meals away from home: daily flat rates where you cannot return home at lunchtime.

Practical tip: even without receipts, fill in these fields actively – the flat rates are not always granted automatically in full.

Family: child deduction, childcare, education

Families have several levers: per child there is a child deduction (around CHF 6,800 at federal level, varying by canton) for as long as the child is a minor or in initial education. On top comes the third-party childcare deduction: proven costs for day-care, childminders or after-school care – up to CHF 25,800 per child at federal level (under 14), also significantly raised in many cantons.

Education costs for adult children (university, apprenticeship) are deductible too, as are maintenance payments. Married dual-earners additionally benefit from the dual-earner deduction (until individual taxation is introduced): 50% of the lower earned income, minimum CHF 8,600 and maximum CHF 14,100.

Premiums, donations and mortgages

Health-insurance premiums and life insurance: deductible as a flat rate at federal level – CHF 1,800 for single persons, CHF 3,600 for married couples, plus CHF 700 per child; often more generous at cantonal level. Also deductible: debt interest (e.g. mortgage interest in full).

Donations: voluntary contributions to recognised charitable organisations are deductible – up to CHF 10,300 for federal tax. Requirement: the organisation is tax-exempt and you have a donation certificate.

Homeowners: besides mortgage interest, maintenance costs, building insurance premiums and energy-saving investments are deductible. Large renovations pay off tax-wise if you spread them over two tax years (progression smoothing).

Deadlines & mistakes that cost you money

The most common mistakes: (1) paying into pillar 3a too late, (2) forgetting form 110 – without declaring your interest you will not get the 35% withholding tax back, (3) missing the deadline – tax returns are generally due by 31 March; an extension is easily requested online in most cantons, (4) throwing away receipts – for effective deductions: no receipts, no deductions.

Also use the right to appeal: if the assessment is wrong you can file an objection within 30 days – free of charge. And from 2026: individual taxation was accepted by voters (8 March 2026); it will be introduced by 2032 at the latest, after which the marriage penalty disappears.
