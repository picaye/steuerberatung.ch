What happened in 2026: every CS account is now a UBS account

On 18 March 2026 UBS confirmed by media release that, with the migration of the Swiss-booked clients, all former Credit Suisse clients globally have been transferred to UBS infrastructure – around 1.2 million clients in total. The integration is expected to be substantially complete by the end of 2026.

In legal terms, Credit Suisse (Schweiz) AG ceased to exist on 1 July 2024, when it merged into UBS Switzerland AG, which succeeded to all its rights and obligations. The technical migration of client relationships followed through 2025 and the first quarter of 2026, each over a weekend and announced by letter.

For your taxes this means: tax year 2026 is the first year in which all former CS clients hold a UBS depot at year-end. Depending on your migration date, 2026 was split into a CS partial period and a UBS partial period – with concrete consequences for statements, income and declaration.

Tax year 2026: one return, possibly two sets of statements

You file the 2026 tax return during 2027 – wealth tax is assessed on the position at the end of the tax period (the post-numerando method, Art. 17 LHTA). Deadlines are cantonal; in Zurich, for instance, the return is due by 31 March of the following year, with extensions available. If your depot was migrated during 2026, expect two sets of documents:

  • CS partial period (1 January until your migration weekend): final CS account statements, interest and dividend advices, any closing statements from the former CS unit.
  • UBS partial period (from migration to 31 December): UBS account statements and the 2026 UBS tax statement / year-end documents.

The return itself remains a single filing: you declare the full year's income and your wealth as of 31 December 2026, regardless of which entity generated it. Use the statements from both partial periods to check whether the UBS 2026 tax statement covers the whole year or whether you must add CS income from the first weeks of January separately; if in doubt, ask your UBS client adviser before filing.

Securities schedule and tax statement: what changed

The annual tax statement now comes in UBS format. The declaration rules are unchanged:

  • Listed securities: ISIN and security number stay the same – the instruments are identical, only the custodian changed. Declare them in the securities schedule as before.
  • Tax value as of 31.12.2026: the relevant figure is the closing rate of the last trading day in December per the Federal Tax Administration's rate lists (ICTax); these rates count as the tax value at 31 December (Art. 14 and 17 para. 1 LHTA). Cantonal tax software links to the rate list – entering the security number is usually enough.
  • No taxable event: the migration was not a sale. Even where instruments were transferred: capital gains on movable private assets are exempt from federal income tax (Art. 16 para. 3 FITA), and your acquisition costs remain valid for any later disposal – carry them over from your old CS statements.

Holding additional positions with neobrokers or foreign banks? The same declaration rules apply – see our guide Foreign depot & neobroker in the tax return.

Practical tip: reconcile the position list and the tax statement item by item. If individual products received new numbers during the migration (a few special products only), clarify the mapping before filing.

Old CS documents: the download window has closed

The UBS transfer FAQ stated that documents of accounts closed and settled before the transfer were not migrated. They were available for download in Credit Suisse Online & Mobile Banking only until three months after the transfer – explicitly including tax-relevant documents such as pillar 3a payment confirmations and interest statements on accounts and mortgages. CS e-banking itself also remained accessible for document retrieval for only three months after migration.

Since the last migrations took place in March 2026, that window is now closed for all clients. In practice:

  1. Take stock now: check which CS tax documents 2024–2026 you have already secured (PDF or paper) – in particular 3a payment confirmations, interest statements and income statements.
  2. Order what is missing: request missing documents via your UBS client adviser or customer service – UBS Switzerland AG is the legal successor of the former CS unit.
  3. Keep them: retain personal tax documents at least until the assessment is final; business assets are subject to the ten-year retention duty.

New IBAN: steering payments and deductions correctly from 2027

With the migration you received new UBS account numbers and IBANs – the old CS numbers could not be carried over for technical reasons. According to the UBS FAQ:

  • payments arriving on your old CS IBAN are forwarded to your new UBS accounts for at least 24 months;
  • existing standing orders and LSV direct debits were transferred to the new UBS accounts automatically;
  • for the migration month you received two separate fee invoices – one up to the transfer, one for the rest of the month. Keep both: third-party asset management costs on movable private assets are deductible.

Do not rely on the forwarding service alone: actively inform payment partners (employer, landlord, health insurer, pillar 3a foundation, charities) of your new IBAN. The tax-relevant rule: the payment record is the deduction record. Pillar 3a contributions, donations and maintenance payments must verifiably have flowed from your account to the correct recipient – make sure such payments run via the new UBS IBAN from 2027 and that you hold the recipients' confirmations (e.g. from the 3a foundation).

Withholding tax: reclaim the 35 %, keep bank details current

Interest and dividends from Swiss sources remain subject to 35 % withholding tax – now deducted by UBS. You reclaim it through ordinary declaration in your tax return: fully declared income means the withholding tax is reimbursed with the assessment. Details in our guide Swiss withholding tax: the 35 percent.

Two points matter especially after the migration:

  1. Declare both partial periods: missing CS weeks from January to March 2026 otherwise create discrepancies between your return and bank reports.
  2. Check your bank details: provide your current UBS account for payouts (e.g. withholding-tax refunds) and replace old CS details in any forms or with authorities. Also mind the refund deadline: claims not filed in time are lost.

Phishing in the name of the migration: how to spot fraud

Large migrations are peak season for fraudsters. The UBS FAQ is explicit: to prepare the transfer, the bank contacted clients exclusively by letter or via (former) Credit Suisse Online & Mobile Banking. Neither UBS nor the tax authorities will ever ask you by e-mail, SMS or phone to disclose personal data or transfer money to another account.

Now that the migration is complete, be extra wary of fake "migration" or "refund" messages asking you to "confirm your new UBS credentials". Our guide Phishing with tax assessments and AGOV shows how these schemes work. Golden rule: always log in directly via the official address and check whether a letter contains your personal client number.

In short

  • Since 18 March 2026 all former CS clients run on UBS systems (about 1.2 million clients globally); the integration should be substantially complete by end-2026.
  • The 2026 tax return (filed in early 2027, cantonal deadlines) is the first return with a UBS depot as of the 31.12.2026 valuation date.
  • Migration ≠ sale: no taxable event, listed ISINs unchanged, acquisition costs carry over; private capital gains are tax-free (Art. 16 para. 3 FITA).
  • Collect statements for the 2026 CS partial period; the 3-month download window for old CS documents has closed – order anything missing directly from UBS.
  • Communicate your new IBAN: old CS IBANs are forwarded for at least 24 months, but the payment record is the deduction record (3a, donations, maintenance).
  • Mind phishing: UBS contacted clients exclusively by letter or e-banking – never disclose data on request by mail/SMS/phone.
  • This is general information, not individual advice – have your specific case reviewed by a professional.
