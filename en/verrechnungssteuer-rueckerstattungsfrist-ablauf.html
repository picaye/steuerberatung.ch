Swiss withholding tax 2023: reclaims expire on 31 December 2026

Why the clock is ticking: the three-year forfeiture period

Every dividend and interest payment from Swiss sources is charged 35 % withholding tax at source (Art. 13 of the Federal Act on Withholding Tax, WHTA/VStG). The bank or company retains the deduction and remits it to the Federal Tax Administration (FTA/ESTV). The money is only returned on request – and the right to request it expires: the refund claim lapses if no application is made within three years after the end of the calendar year in which the taxable payment fell due (Art. 32 para. 1 WHTA).

For 2023 income, that deadline is 31 December 2026. The FTA states expressly that this is a forfeiture period which, as a rule, can be neither interrupted nor extended. Dividends or interest from 2023 that were never declared – and for which nothing is done by year-end – are lost permanently.

The next countdowns follow in yearly rhythm: 2024 income until 31 December 2027, 2025 income until 31 December 2028. Once you have understood the pattern, the check becomes an end-of-year routine.

Practical tip: Put 31 December in your calendar as a fixed review date – every tax period carries its own expiry date, and none of them moves.

The easy route: declaring it in your tax return

If you are assessed under the ordinary procedure, you have effectively already filed the claim: declaring the income in the securities schedule of your cantonal tax return acts as the refund request with your canton of residence (Art. 30 WHTA). The tax authority credits the withholding tax automatically in the assessment – provided the income is declared in full, gross, including the 35 % withheld.

The classic failure case is portfolio income simply missing from the return: overlooked securities, forgotten dividends from small holdings, or accounts nobody expected the tax office to see. With the automatic exchange of information (AEOI) in place with most partner states, such gaps surface regularly in follow-up assessments – and without a declaration there is no refund.

Keep the two workstreams apart: a belatedly filed 2023 return can still recover the withholding tax within the deadline, but it triggers back taxation on the previously untaxed income. For past concealment, the first voluntary disclosure of tax evasion is penalty-free: only back tax plus default interest remain owed (Art. 175 para. 3 of the Federal Act on Direct Federal Tax); on any further disclosure the penalty is reduced to one fifth of the tax evaded (Art. 175 para. 4). If the constellation is unclear, have it reviewed before filing.

Practical tip: Pull the 2023 year-end and dividend statements from every bank and platform you have ever used – including accounts you barely touch. The gross list of due payments shows at a glance whether every security made it onto your return.

Without automatic assessment: the direct refund procedure

The automatic credit via assessment does not cover every case: those who do not file a tax return, who moved abroad or died in the meantime, or who received income that never appeared in any assessment must apply in writing to the FTA – most easily via the ESTV portal or the electronic form 25 «Application for refund of withholding tax» (paper forms are no longer available; an application may be filed no earlier than after the end of the calendar year in which the payment became due – Art. 29 WHTA). Attach proof of the taxable payment, such as the year-end portfolio statement.

The application must be filed by 31 December 2026. The FTA does not confirm receipt of refund applications; applications submitted online can be tracked at any time in the portal. Filing electronically with a retrievable reference before the holiday season is the safer course.

For estates: the forfeiture period keeps running on a deceased person's income. Whoever administers an estate should review the depots for open withholding-tax claims of the last three years before they expire one by one.

Practical tip: Keep the portal extract or a copy of the application with its filing date until the refund is complete – in a dispute, only proof that the claim was filed before 31 December counts.

What the deadline does not fix: the neighbouring pots

Not everything that looks like 35 % is Swiss withholding tax: it applies only to taxable Swiss-source payments – dividends, profit shares, interest and certain monetised benefits from closely held companies. Income from foreign securities bears no Swiss withholding tax; there the issue is foreign withholding tax, credited via the DA-1 schedule of your tax return – a different regime with different rules.

Second, every refund presupposes that the income is declared and taxed. Under a correct assessment the withholding tax is fully credited; concealing the income simply lets the money lie where it is – while adding back-tax risk on top.

Third, for collective investment schemes the refund of withholding tax charged at fund level generally runs through the fund management, not through the individual holder of fund units in private wealth. And a depot opened only in 2024 has no 2023 question at all – though you should still review older accounts, savings capital and interest-bearing policies.

Practical tip: Keep the pots separate: 35 % Swiss withholding tax (2023 income: claim by 31.12.2026), foreign withholding tax (DA-1, no WHTA deadline), fund assets (via the fund management) – three regimes, three checklists.

Quick check: the size of the stake

A mid-sized portfolio with CHF 20,000 of gross 2023 dividend and interest income carried CHF 7,000 of withholding tax. If it was never declared and no claim is filed by 31 December 2026, those CHF 7,000 are gone – regardless of the income tax that is owed on the earnings anyway.

The inverse: declared in the 2023 return (or a belatedly filed 2023 assessment before the deadline), the CHF 7,000 are credited; income tax on the same earnings is due, but the net position is clearly better than forfeiting. For a married couple with one joint depot the workload hardly doubles – the potential refund does.

Do the arithmetic before deciding: gross income, tax withheld, expected income-tax effect, and whether back tax is on the table. The effort for a portal application is modest; the amounts often are not.

Practical tip: Start with the portfolios you no longer manage – children's accounts, old vested-profit and savings accounts, interest-bearing insurance policies. The most common forfeitures concern securities nobody thought of after the last bank switch.

In short

  • Art. 32 para. 1 WHTA: refund claims for 2023 dividends and interest expire on 31 December 2026 – a forfeiture period, as a rule neither interruptible nor extendable.
  • Ordinarily assessed taxpayers declare gross income in the securities schedule; the declaration itself is the refund request (Art. 30 WHTA).
  • Direct claim to the FTA: ESTV portal or electronic form 25; filing possible from the year after the payment became due (Art. 29 WHTA), must arrive before 31.12.2026.
  • Foreign withholding tax is a different regime (DA-1); fund-level refunds run through the fund management.
  • Next deadlines: 2024 income by 31.12.2027, 2025 income by 31.12.2028.
